# Arrow of Light: required adventures (DRAFT 1, 40-minute den time)

Researched 2026-09-30 by the cubmaster-program agent, using the format of Webelos draft 2. Every plan here is in our own words. Requirements are only summarized, so use the official page for the full text. Anything marked "(check)" still needs confirming. Nothing is in the app yet.

How these plans fit a Pack 569 den night:
- The pack does the opening ceremony together: flag, Pledge, Oath and Law. Each den then splits off for about 40 minutes. The Arrow of Light den leads the opening flag ceremony when its turn comes in the normal rotation.
- Den meetings here have no gathering or opening steps. Each one is den steps plus one short den closing. The minutes add up to 35–40 and never go over 40, so the timer can use them as they are.
- If the pack opening already covers a requirement, the meeting doesn't get a step for it. Instead it has a "Done at the pack opening:" line, and a leader confirms each scout took part.
- In the same way, if a pack meeting covers a requirement (Duty to God's faith-based guest, at one of the pack's guest-speaker nights), the adventure has a "Done at a pack meeting:" line and a den-level Option B. The den leader confirms who was there.
- Where the choice belongs to the den leader, the guide says "Leader's choice" and gives Option A / Option B.
- Outings (the troop visit, the service day, the campout, the paramedic visit) aren't normal den nights. They keep their own length and are marked "(outing, about N min)". Every outing goes through the council outing process (check).
- "Reqs: N" means the step does real work on that requirement. "Reqs: N (set-up)" means the step only sets up or reinforces it and does not complete it. Some steps use a semicolon to list both kinds, for example "Reqs: 7; 5 (set-up)".
- The guides don't tick requirements. Leaders record progress on the app's Advancement board afterward.
- Arrow of Light scouts are in 5th grade, about 10–11. Treat them as almost Scouts BSA. The official pages call the den a **patrol**, and in this guide "patrol" and "den" mean the same group. The elected **patrol leader** is the AoL version of the denner. Patrol leaders and other scouts run steps and lead discussions, but the official Patrol Elections page says an AoL patrol leader is never put in charge of other scouts, and the den leader leads the den.
- AoL scouts come without an adult partner, so every den room needs two registered adults 21 or over, and there is no one-on-one contact. That includes guests and visiting troop members. If any girl attends, one of the two registered adults is a woman 21 or over.
- On 2026-09-30 I read all six official AoL adventure pages on scouting.org and the activity pages linked below. Plain WebFetch truncates scouting.org, so I used the r.jina.ai reader. I cross-checked everything against the Michigan Crossroads Council's Oct 2024 Arrow of Light requirements PDF (https://michiganscouting.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Arrow-of-Light-Adventure-Requirements.pdf). The six required names match the app's `ADVENTURES['Arrow of Light'].required` and are in category order.
- The 2024 AoL program is very different from the old one. Lists showing adventures like "Scouting Adventure," "Duty to God in Action," "Building a Better World" or "Camper" are from before 2024. Use the scouting.org links.
- Knives and fire:
  - Knives (Keith's rule): a scout who has earned their rank's knife adventure this year (AoL **Knife Safety**) may use a knife under supervision at home, campouts and outings, but never at den meetings, where adults do the cutting. The official Scout Basic Essentials page also says Knife Safety comes before carrying a pocketknife.
  - Fire (Keith's rule): an AoL scout lights a fire the same way as a Webelos, at the den leader's choice. Option A: an adult lights it. Option B: the scout lights it with an adult at their elbow. Adults carry the matches. The **Firem'n Chit** is a Scouts BSA award only, so AoL scouts don't earn it first.
  - None of the six required adventures below includes knife work or requires a scout to light a fire.

## Bobcat (Arrow of Light)
- Character & Leadership · Meetings: 3 (two den meetings plus one troop-visit outing) · Official page: https://www.scouting.org/cub-scout-adventures/bobcat-aol/ · Other sources: https://michiganscouting.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Arrow-of-Light-Adventure-Requirements.pdf, https://www.scouting.org/cub-scout-activities/patrol-elections/, https://www.scouting.org/cub-scout-activities/patrol-flag-arrow-of-light/, https://www.scouting.org/cub-scout-activities/scouts-bsa-sign-salute-and-handshake/, https://www.scouting.org/cub-scout-activities/scouts-bsa-slogan-motto/, https://www.scouting.org/cub-scout-activities/scouts-bsa-troop-meeting-scavenger-hunt/, https://www.scouting.org/cub-scout-activities/scouts-bsa-troop-daytime-activity/, https://www.scouting.org/cub-scout-activities/scouts-bsa-troop-overnight/, https://www.scouting.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Troop-Visits-for-Arrow-of-Light-Scouts-.pdf, https://scoutermom.com/45167/arrow-of-light-bobcat-adventure/ · Checked: 2026-09-30
- Summary: Bobcat comes first at every rank. At AoL it turns the den into a patrol. Scouts elect a patrol leader, pick a patrol name, write a Code of Conduct, learn the **Scouts BSA** sign, salute, handshake, slogan and motto, and visit a Scouts BSA troop. The troop visit is **Req 7** and is an outing. The Roundtable guide above says AoL scouts should visit a troop "right away," at the start of the year, which makes it the first crossover step. Scouts say the Oath and Law at the pack opening.
- Requirements (own words):
  1. Pick a patrol name, elect a patrol leader, discuss why patrols work. (meeting)
  2. Get to know the members of your patrol. (meeting)
  3. Say the Scout Oath and Law with your patrol. (pack opening, if the den stands together with its den leader)
  4. Make a patrol Code of Conduct together. (meeting)
  5. Show the Scouts BSA sign, salute and handshake, and their uses. (meeting)
  6. Learn the Scouts BSA slogan and motto. (meeting)
  7. Visit a Scouts BSA troop with your patrol or parent. (outing)
  8. Do the Parent's Guide youth-protection activities with a parent. (at home)
- Leader's choice for Req 7:
  - Option A · the patrol visits together: the whole den goes to a troop meeting (Meeting 3 below), or to a troop daytime activity or troop overnight (official options).
  - Option B · each family visits with their scout: families pick a troop and go with their scout. The den leader gives each family the scavenger-hunt sheet and the questions from Meeting 2 and confirms the visit afterward. This works well for families already comparing troops.
- Safety notes:
  - Req 8 is for families only. Leaders send the link and never run it themselves.
  - Two registered adults in the den room, and no one-on-one contact. If any girl attends, one of the two registered adults is a woman 21 or over.
  - The patrol leader is never put in charge of other scouts (official Patrol Elections page).
  - The troop visit is an outing. It needs two registered adults, permission, a Scouting America Annual Health and Medical Record for every youth and adult (official troop-visit activities), and the council outing process (check). If any girl attends, one of the two registered adults is a woman 21 or over. Tell the troop's leader about any medical needs.
  - A troop overnight is camping: AoL camping rules apply (official Bobcat page), including a BALOO-trained adult and each scout attending with a parent or guardian (Guide to Safe Scouting, see Outdoor Adventurer).

### Meeting 1 of 3 · Patrol Kickoff · 40 min
- Done at the pack opening: Req 3. The den stands together with its den leader, and a leader confirms each scout took part.
- Prep: 1–2 weeks before, email families the Parent's Guide link for Req 8. Decide what the patrol leader does and for how long (for example, one month, then a new election), as the official Patrol Elections activity asks. Cut ballot slips. Bring a beach ball and write get-to-know questions on it with a marker ("best trip ever," "one food you'd never eat," "a skill you want to learn this year"). Ask the Cubmaster whether the pack buys patrol emblem patches (check). Confirm your second registered adult.
- Supplies: beach ball with questions, ballot slips, pencils, chart paper, markers, a list of the patrol leader's jobs
- Tell parents before they leave: Please do the Parent's Guide activities with your scout at home (Req 8), and tell the den leader when you're done. This year we visit a Scouts BSA troop (Req 7). The date and troop are coming soon, and parents are welcome to come.
1. **Beach Ball Toss** · den · 10 min · Reqs: 2
   - Say: "Catch the ball, read the question under your right thumb, answer it, and throw it to someone who hasn't had a turn."
   - How: 1) Stand in a **circle**. 2) Each scout **answers** the question under their thumb. 3) Go around **twice**. 4) Finish with each scout **naming one thing** they learned about someone else.
   - Tip: **Leaders play too.** It shows scouts that talking to adults in Scouting is normal, which matters at the troop visit.
2. **Why Patrols?** · den · 7 min · Reqs: 1
   - Say: "Scouts BSA troops are built from patrols: small teams that plan, cook, camp and solve problems together. Why would a small team work better than one big group?"
   - How: 1) Take **answers**, such as everyone gets a job, friends help each other, and decisions are faster. 2) Write them on **chart paper**. 3) Explain that this year **our den is a patrol**.
   - Tip: **Keep the chart.** It covers the "discuss the benefits" part of Req 1. Note who took part.
3. **Patrol Leader Election** · den · 10 min · Reqs: 1
   - Say: "Here's what our patrol leader does and for how long. Want to run? Raise your hand and tell us in one minute why you'd be good at it."
   - How: 1) Read the **job list**, and make clear the den leader still leads the den. 2) Candidates give **one-minute talks**. 3) Hold a **secret ballot**. 4) Two scouts **count** with a leader watching. 5) **Announce** the winner.
   - Tip: **No hurt feelings:** Say up front that there will be more elections, so more scouts get a turn.
4. **Name Our Patrol** · den · 8 min · Reqs: 1
   - Say: "Patrol leader, your first job is to run this: we need a patrol name."
   - How: 1) The **patrol leader** takes ideas, and a leader writes them down. 2) Narrow the list to **three**. 3) **Vote**. 4) Practice a **patrol yell** if there's time.
   - Tip: **Official option:** Making a patrol flag (Patrol Flag AoL activity) is a good make-up or early-arrival project. Early set-up starts only when both registered adults are there; the scout's parent stays until then.
5. **Clean-Up & Handoff** · closing · 5 min · Reqs: none
   - Say: "Patrol leader, call clean-up and lead us out."
   - How: 1) **Two-minute clean-up**. 2) **Handshake line** past the leaders. 3) Hand each scout to their **adult** by name.
   - Tip: **Pickup:** Don't let a scout leave until you've seen their adult.
   - (at home) Req 8: families do the Parent's Guide activities.

### Meeting 2 of 3 · Code, Sign and Slogan · 40 min
- Done at the pack opening: Req 3 for any scout who missed it at Meeting 1. A leader confirms each of those scouts took part.
- Prep: 1–2 weeks before, confirm the troop and the date for Req 7, collect permission slips and health forms, and start the council outing process (check). Print the official Troop Scavenger Hunt sheet (https://filestore.scouting.org/filestore/cubscouts/adventure-resources/aol/Arrow%20of%20Light%20Bobcat%207%20Scouts%20BSA%20Troop%20Scavenger%20Hunt.pdf), one per scout. Write "[Patrol name] Code" at the top of a poster board. Make six index cards: three with a picture of each move and three with each move's use.
- Supplies: poster board, markers, index cards, pencils, scavenger-hunt sheets, permission slips
- Tell parents before they leave: The troop visit is [date, time, place] (check). Please return the permission slip and health form. Parents are welcome, and it's a good way to start thinking about which troop your scout will join.
1. **Our Patrol Code** · den · 12 min · Reqs: 4
   - Say: "Troops run on rules the scouts agree to. Let's write ours, and say what we *will* do."
   - How: 1) The **patrol leader** runs the brainstorm, and a volunteer writes. 2) Turn each "don't" into a **"do."** 3) **Vote** on 5–6 rules. 4) Everyone **signs**.
   - Tip: **Link to the Law:** Ask which Scout Law point each rule matches.
2. **Scouts BSA Sign, Salute & Handshake** · den · 10 min · Reqs: 5
   - Say: "You've used the Cub Scout versions for years. Troops use different ones, and now you learn them."
   - How: 1) **Show the differences**:
      - Sign: right arm up with the elbow bent square, three middle fingers up, and the thumb holding the little finger down.
      - Salute: make the sign, then bring the forefinger to the hat brim or right eyebrow.
      - Handshake: a normal handshake, but with the **left** hand.

     2) Split into **two teams** for a relay. The runner flips a card, then does the move or says its use: the sign for the Oath and Law and to ask for quiet, the salute for the flag and to greet Scouts and leaders, the handshake to greet Scouts and leaders. 3) Each scout **does all three and says each use** to a leader.
   - Tip: **Three fingers, not two:** Watch for old habits. Ask the Cubmaster whether AoL scouts use the Scouts BSA sign and salute at pack flag ceremonies from now on (check).
3. **Slogan & Motto Word Circle** · den · 7 min · Reqs: 6
   - Say: "Troop slogan: Do a Good Turn Daily. Troop motto: Be Prepared. Now we say them one word at a time, but no one knows who's next."
   - How: 1) **Circle up**. 2) Any scout can say the **next word**, but if two talk at once, **start over**. 3) Count the **clean rounds**. 4) Ask for **one example** of each: a good turn you could do tomorrow, and a way to be prepared for the campout.
   - Tip: **Different from Cub Scouts:** The official activity points out that these differ from the Cub Scout slogan and motto. Ask scouts to name the Cub Scout ones for contrast (check the wording against the AoL handbook).
4. **Troop Visit Prep** · den · 6 min · Reqs: 7 (set-up)
   - Say: "At the troop, you're detectives. What do you want to find out?"
   - How: 1) Hand out the **scavenger-hunt sheets**. 2) Each scout writes **two questions**. The official activity suggests asking how often they meet, how big the troop is, what summer camp is like and how merit badges work. 3) Go over **visit manners**: the Scouts BSA handshake, listen during their meeting, stay with the patrol.
   - Tip: **Sheets stay in the den box** until the visit.
5. **Read the Code & Handoff** · closing · 5 min · Reqs: none
   - Say: "Patrol leader, read our Code, and then call clean-up."
   - How: 1) **Read the Code** together. 2) **Clean up**. 3) **Hand off** each scout to their adult, putting the **permission slip** in the parent's hand.
   - Tip: **Keep it visible:** Roll up the Code and pack it with the den box.

### Meeting 3 of 3 · Visit a Scouts BSA Troop (outing, about 90 min)
- Option B: skip this meeting. Each family visits a troop on its own and tells the den leader which troop and when. The scout brings the completed scavenger-hunt sheet to the next den meeting.
- Prep: 1–2 weeks before, confirm with the troop's Scoutmaster what the troop has planned and what scouts should bring (Six Essentials, uniform). Collect permission slips and health forms, give the troop leader any medical information, confirm two registered adults, and follow the council outing process (check). Send families the address, the time and a pickup plan. The chase driver never carries a lone scout who isn't their own child. Call the scout's parent, add a second adult, or carry a buddy too.
- Supplies: scavenger-hunt sheets, pencils, clipboards, health forms, the den's question list
- Tell parents before they leave: Ask your scout what surprised them about the troop. If you're comparing troops, the official daytime-activity page links an article on choosing a troop, so ask the den leader for it.
1. **Arrive & Check-In** · den · 10 min · Reqs: 7 (set-up)
   - Say: "Stay with the patrol. Greet the troop scouts with the left handshake."
   - How: 1) **Sign in** each scout. 2) Do a **restroom stop** in buddy pairs, with an adult waiting outside. 3) Introduce the patrol to the **troop's senior patrol leader** or Scoutmaster.
   - Tip: **Headcount** before you walk in.
2. **Watch the Troop Meeting** · den · 60 min · Reqs: 7
   - Say: "Watch how the troop runs itself. Mark your sheet every time you spot something on it."
   - How: 1) Scouts **join in** whatever the troop invites them to do. 2) They **fill in the scavenger hunt** as they go. 3) Both registered adults stay **with the patrol** the whole time.
   - Tip: **Scouts BSA youth lead their meetings.** Point it out quietly. It's the biggest difference from Cub Scouts.
3. **Ask the Scouts** · den · 10 min · Reqs: 7
   - Say: "Now's your chance. Ask your questions."
   - How: 1) Troop scouts **answer questions** from the patrol, in a group and never one-on-one. 2) Each AoL scout asks **at least one question**.
   - Tip: **Parents too:** Parents who came can talk with the Scoutmaster separately.
4. **Patrol Debrief** · den · 5 min · Reqs: 7
   - Say: "One thing you liked, and one thing that was different from Cub Scouts."
   - How: 1) **Huddle** outside or in a corner. 2) Everyone **shares one of each**. 3) Note who **finished Req 7**.
   - Tip: **Thank-you:** The patrol leader leads a thank-you to the troop.
5. **Headcount & Handoff** · closing · 5 min · Reqs: none
   - Say: "Buddy check, then find your adult."
   - How: 1) **Count off**. 2) Hand each scout to their **adult** by name.
   - Tip: **Keep the contact:** Ask the troop about other events AoL scouts can join, like a campout (see Outdoor Adventurer Option B) or a service project (see Citizenship).

## Citizenship (Arrow of Light)
- Citizenship · Meetings: 3 (two den meetings plus one service outing) · Official page: https://www.scouting.org/cub-scout-adventures/citizenship/ · Other sources: https://michiganscouting.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Arrow-of-Light-Adventure-Requirements.pdf, https://www.scouting.org/cub-scout-activities/service-project-planning/, https://www.scouting.org/cub-scout-activities/10000-for-the-troops-arrow-of-light/, https://www.scouting.org/cub-scout-activities/aol-scouting-for-food/, https://www.scouting.org/cub-scout-activities/scouts-bsa-troop-service-project/, https://www.scouting.org/cub-scout-activities/helping-neighbors/, https://www.scouting.org/cub-scout-activities/little-free-library/, https://filestore.scouting.org/filestore/healthsafety/pdf/680-027.pdf, https://filestore.scouting.org/filestore/healthsafety/pdf/680-028.pdf · Checked: 2026-09-30
- Summary: AoL Citizenship has two requirements: plan a safe service project, and serve for at least **two hours** in total. The Service Project Planning activity says the planned project doesn't have to be the one the patrol actually does. The two hours can be spread over several projects, so this plan counts about 26 minutes of holiday-card service at a den meeting plus about 100 minutes at a service outing. There is no elected-official or flag requirement at AoL.
- Requirements (own words):
  1. Pick a patrol or pack service project; plan it using SAFE. (meeting)
  2. Do at least two hours of service, in one or more projects. (meeting and outing)
- Leader's choice for the service outing (Meeting 3):
  - Option A · the patrol's own project: carry out the project the patrol planned at Meeting 1, if it passed the SAFE check and fits a 2-hour window.
  - Option B · join a project that's already running: a Scouts BSA troop's service project (official, and a good crossover link) or the council's Scouting for Food (official; check NEGA's date).
- Safety notes:
  - The official page points to the Service Project Planning Checklist and SAFE Project Tool Use (links above). Train scouts before they use any tool, and an adult supervises the whole time.
  - Tools follow the SAFE Project Tool Use chart (680-028): AoL scouts may use rakes, shovels and trowels; wheelbarrows, handsaws, electric screwdrivers and palm sanders are Scouts BSA only, so adults use them.
  - The service outing needs two registered adults, permission, health forms, a transport plan and the council outing process (check). If any girl attends, one of the two registered adults is a woman 21 or over. The chase driver never carries a lone scout who isn't their own child. Call the scout's parent, add a second adult, or carry a buddy too. Scouting for Food needs an Activity Consent Form for each scout (https://filestore.scouting.org/filestore/pdf/19-673.pdf), buddy pairs, and a reminder that it's illegal to put bags in or on mailboxes (official activity).
  - Holiday cards: no glitter (official activity page). Scouts sign with their first name only.
  - Snacks at the outing: each scout brings their own, and there's no sharing because of allergies.

### Meeting 1 of 3 · Plan a Safe Service Project · 40 min
- Prep: 1–2 weeks before, print the SAFE Service Project Planning Checklist (680-027), one per scout. Bring chart paper and markers. Know which outing option you'll offer, so you can steer the vote toward something the patrol can really do.
- Supplies: checklists, pencils, chart paper, markers, sticky dots for voting
- Tell parents before they leave: Your scout helped plan a service project using Scouting's SAFE Checklist (Req 1). Our service day is [date/place] (check). Every scout needs two hours of service in total, and we'll track the minutes.
1. **What Needs Doing?** · den · 8 min · Reqs: 1
   - Say: "Look around your school, your street, your town. What needs fixing, cleaning, collecting or building that our patrol or our pack could actually do?"
   - How: 1) The **patrol leader** runs the brainstorm. 2) A scribe writes **every idea**. Examples are a park clean-up, a food drive, a Little Free Library, helping a neighbor with yard work, or cards for service members. 3) Each scout puts **two sticky dots** on favorites.
   - Tip: **Doable beats huge:** Ask, "Could we finish this in one Saturday morning?"
2. **Plan the Steps** · den · 12 min · Reqs: 1
   - Say: "We've picked one. Now what has to happen first, second, third?"
   - How: 1) Take the **top idea**. 2) The patrol lists the **steps**: permission, supplies, who does what, when, and how everyone gets there. 3) Scouts copy the project onto **their own checklist**.
   - Tip: **Official note:** The plan doesn't have to be the project you actually do. Planning it well is what Req 1 asks.
3. **SAFE Check** · den · 15 min · Reqs: 1
   - Say: "Scouting checks four things before any activity. S is Supervision, A is Assessment, F is Fitness and skill, E is Equipment and environment. Does our plan pass?"
   - How: 1) Scouts **take turns reading** each letter on the checklist. 2) For each one, the patrol writes **how the plan meets it**, such as which adults, what could go wrong, who can do each job, and what tools and gear. 3) If a point **fails**, change the plan or pick another project, as the official activity says. 4) Each scout **explains one letter** to a leader.
   - Tip: **Tools:** If the plan uses tools, open the SAFE Project Tool Use sheet and check which tools are allowed at this age.
4. **Patrol Leader Wrap-Up & Handoff** · closing · 5 min · Reqs: none
   - Say: "Patrol leader, tell us our project in one sentence. Everybody else, clean-up."
   - How: 1) The patrol leader gives a **one-sentence summary**. 2) **Clean up**. 3) **Hand off** each scout to their adult.
   - Tip: **Keep the checklists:** Photograph them for the den file, then send them home.

### Meeting 2 of 3 · Cards for the Troops · 40 min
- Prep: 1–2 weeks before, check the official activity page again (https://www.scouting.org/cub-scout-activities/10000-for-the-troops-arrow-of-light/). On 2026-09-30 it said:
  - Cards are no bigger than letter paper folded in half.
  - No glitter.
  - Open with "Dear Warrior" and write a greeting that suits people of any belief.
  - Add a personal note about how your family celebrates.
  - Sign with your first name, your town and state, and the pack number.
  - The den mails its own cards to: 10,000 for the Troops, Scouting America, 1325 West Walnut Hill Lane, Irving, TX 75015-2079.
  - Mail them by the ship-by date, [date] (2026: Nov 6).

  This meeting must happen early enough for an adult to mail the cards by then. If Meeting 1 can't happen first, hold this meeting first. Req 2 doesn't depend on Req 1. Print a **service-minutes log** (one line per scout).
- Supplies: construction paper, copy paper, markers, crayons, stickers, scissors, one large mailing envelope or box, service-minutes log, clock
- Tell parents before they leave: Tonight your scout did about 26 minutes of service, toward the two hours Req 2 needs. The rest comes on our service day, [date] (check). An adult mails the cards before the ship-by date.
1. **Job Briefing** · den · 4 min · Reqs: 2 (set-up)
   - Say: "Service members far from home get cards from Cub Scouts all over the country. Tonight's our turn, and it counts as service."
   - How: 1) Explain **who gets the cards**. 2) Go over the **rules**: "Dear Warrior," first name, town, state and Pack 569, no glitter, no bigger than folded letter paper. 3) Show **one example**.
   - Tip: **Greetings for everyone:** "Happy holidays" and "Thank you for your service" fit any belief.
2. **Holiday Cards for Service Members** · den · 26 min · Reqs: 2
   - Say: "Make each card your own. Tell them how your family celebrates."
   - How: 1) Write the **start time** on the log. 2) Scouts **fold, decorate and write**. 3) Aim for **three cards each**. 4) Write the **end time** on the log.
   - Tip: **Spelling help, not wording help:** The words should be the scout's own.
3. **Log the Minutes** · den · 5 min · Reqs: 2 (set-up)
   - Say: "Real service gets tracked. Write your minutes on the log."
   - How: 1) Each scout writes **their minutes**, counting time spent making cards and not the briefing. 2) A leader **initials** each line. 3) Ask, "**How many more minutes do you need?**"
   - Tip: **Missed tonight?** That scout needs the full two hours at the service day or another project (check).
4. **Pack the Cards & Handoff** · closing · 5 min · Reqs: none
   - Say: "Patrol leader, count the cards into the envelope. Everyone else, clean-up."
   - How: 1) **Count and pack** the cards. 2) **Clean up**. 3) **Hand off** each scout to their adult.
   - Tip: **Photo:** Photograph the stack of cards. Photograph only scouts whose parents gave photo permission; post only on the pack's official channel, with no names.

### Meeting 3 of 3 · Service Day (outing, about 125 min)
- Prep: 1–2 weeks before, confirm the option you chose. For Option A, confirm the site owner's permission, supplies and tool training. For Option B, confirm with the troop or the council what to bring, and collect Activity Consent Forms for Scouting for Food. Collect permission slips and health forms, confirm two registered adults, plan transport, and follow the council outing process (check). The chase driver never carries a lone scout who isn't their own child. Call the scout's parent, add a second adult, or carry a buddy too. Bring the service-minutes log and the Meeting 1 SAFE checklists.
- Supplies: work gloves, trash bags or project supplies, water, first-aid kit, health forms, service-minutes log, clipboards
- Tell parents before they leave: Please check your scout's minutes on the log. Any scout short of two hours can finish at another project (check).
1. **Check-In & SAFE Briefing** · den · 10 min · Reqs: 2 (set-up)
   - Say: "Remember our SAFE check? Here's how it applies today."
   - How: 1) **Sign in**, then **pair up buddies**. 2) Give **tool training** if tools are used. 3) Go over **boundaries** and the whistle signal. 4) Write the **start time** on the log.
   - Tip: **Needs a second adult.** One adult leads the work while the other watches the buddy pairs.
2. **Serve, First Half** · den · 50 min · Reqs: 2
   - Say: "Work with your buddy, and ask before you touch anything you're not sure about."
   - How: 1) Buddy pairs **work their jobs**. 2) An adult **rotates** among the pairs. 3) With a troop, AoL scouts **work alongside troop scouts** and follow their directions (official activity).
   - Tip: **Anything sharp, broken or heavy:** Adults handle it.
3. **Water Break** · den · 5 min · Reqs: none
   - Say: "Water, shade, and your own snack."
   - How: 1) **Drink water**. 2) Eat **your own** snack, with no swaps. 3) **Switch jobs** if the patrol wants.
   - Tip: **Heat:** Take extra breaks in warm weather.
4. **Serve, Second Half** · den · 50 min · Reqs: 2
   - Say: "Let's finish strong and leave it better than we found it."
   - How: 1) **Finish the project**. 2) **Clean up** the work site. 3) Write the **end time** on the log.
   - Tip: **Count real work time only.** Breaks don't count toward the two hours.
5. **Service Reflection** · den · 5 min · Reqs: 2

   - Tip: **Gloves back:** Collect the den's gloves and tools before handoff.

## Outdoor Adventurer (Arrow of Light)
- Outdoors · Meetings: 3 (two den meetings plus one overnight campout) · Official page: https://www.scouting.org/cub-scout-adventures/outdoor-adventurer/ · Other sources: https://michiganscouting.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Arrow-of-Light-Adventure-Requirements.pdf, https://www.scouting.org/cub-scout-activities/scout-basic-essentials/, https://www.scouting.org/cub-scout-activities/scout-basic-essentials-backpack/, https://www.scouting.org/cub-scout-activities/troop-shake-down/, https://www.scouting.org/cub-scout-activities/den-campout/, https://www.scouting.org/cub-scout-activities/troop-campout/, https://www.scouting.org/cub-scout-activities/campsite-setup/, https://www.scouting.org/cub-scout-activities/safe-food-handling-and-clean-up/, https://www.scouting.org/cub-scout-activities/start-stop-continue/, https://www.scouting.org/health-and-safety/gss/gss03/, https://filestore.scouting.org/filestore/HealthSafety/pdf/680-685.pdf, https://filestore.scouting.org/filestore/HealthSafety/pdf/680-001_AB.pdf · Checked: 2026-09-30
- Summary: AoL has no "Let's Camp" elective, because camping is this required adventure. Scouts learn the Scout Basic Essentials, find the campsite on a map, apply the SAFE Checklist, shake down their gear, learn camp food safety and three-pot cleanup, and then camp with their patrol or a Scouts BSA troop. At camp they lay out the site, set up patrol gear before their own tents, keep the kitchen clean, and debrief afterward. The campout dinner can also be the Personal Fitness meal (see that adventure).
- Requirements (own words):
  1. Learn the Scout Basic Essentials. (meeting)
  2. Choose your overnight gear, including a tent, and how to carry it. (meeting, or a troop shakedown outing)
  3. Review the SAFE Checklist's four points and apply them to the campout. (meeting)
  4. Find your campsite on a map. (meeting)
  5. Go on a campout with your patrol or a troop. (outing)
  6. At camp, place kitchen, eating area, tents, firepit; patrol gear first. (outing)
  7. Explain camp food safety and kitchen cleanliness; show it at camp. (explain: meeting; show: outing)
  8. Afterward, discuss with patrol what worked, what to change, Outdoor Code, LNT. (outing, after pack-up)
- Leader's choice for the campout (Req 5):
  - Option A · patrol campout: an AoL den overnight run by the den, or the patrol camping as its own group at a pack campout (check that a pack campout counts as "with your patrol").
  - Option B · camp with a Scouts BSA troop: the official Troop Campout activity. The Guide to Safe Scouting allows it with a troop the scouts are eligible to join, with the AoL scouts as a patrol, two-deep leadership, and all Cub Scout camping rules including BALOO. The troop plans the schedule, and the den leader makes sure Reqs 6–8 happen.
- Leader's choice for Req 2:
  - Option A · den shakedown at Meeting 2, led by an IOLS-trained adult if the pack has one or a local troop can send one (check).
  - Option B · the official Troop Shakedown: the patrol brings its gear to a troop meeting, where troop members review it (an outing, council outing process (check)).
- Safety notes:
  - The official page and the Guide to Safe Scouting require:
    - at least one BALOO-trained adult on the campout
    - the online Hazardous Weather training and the weather safety video
    - a review of the Guide to Safe Scouting's camping section, and "Behavior Around Campfires" if there's a campfire
  - GSS rules for AoL den camping:
    - no more than two nights in a row
    - each scout should come with their own parent or guardian. A scout whose parent can't come may attend under the supervision of at least two registered leaders, if the parent and the den's leaders agree beforehand
    - youth tent only with their own parent or guardian (and siblings), never with another adult
    - a scout without a parent tents alone or with a same-gender scout within two years of age, never with an adult
    - Cub Scout camping is limited to council-designated sites. The app's den-campout page names the council camps Scoutland and Camp Rainey Mountain (check)
  - Health forms: every youth and adult needs the Annual Health and Medical Record, parts A and B (https://filestore.scouting.org/filestore/HealthSafety/pdf/680-001_AB.pdf). Review them for allergies, diet and restrictions.
  - Food: review the Food Allergies safety moment and ask about religious or cultural food needs. **If a scout carries an EpiPen, at least one other adult must know how to use it** (official page). Follow the Keep Your Food Safe guide.
  - Knives: a pocketknife is one of the Basic Essentials, but an AoL scout must earn **Knife Safety** before carrying one (official Scout Basic Essentials page). In this adventure, scouts only look at a closed knife held by an adult. A scout who has earned Knife Safety this year may use a knife under supervision at the campout, but never at den meetings, where adults do the cutting. Scouts who haven't earned Knife Safety leave their knives at home.
  - Fire: an AoL scout lights a fire the same way as a Webelos, at the den leader's choice. Option A: an adult lights it. Option B: the scout lights it with an adult at their elbow. Adults carry the matches. The Firem'n Chit is a Scouts BSA award only. Adults tend any fire, the firepit is a safe distance from tents, and there's a water bucket at the fire and every stove.
  - Cooking: an adult stands at every stove. Three-pot water is hot (the official activity says at least 160°F), so adults pour it and scouts use tongs.
  - Site hazards (official Campsite Setup activity): no dead trees or hanging limbs over tents, no low spots that flood, and no lone trees or hilltops in lightning. Postpone or shelter for lightning.
  - Restrooms: buddies go together, and an adult waits outside. No one-on-one contact.
  - If any girl attends, one of the two registered adults is a woman 21 or over.

### Meeting 1 of 3 · Essentials, Map and SAFE · 40 min
- Prep: 1–2 weeks before, book the campsite (Option A) or confirm the troop campout (Option B), and start the council outing process (check). Gather the eleven Scout Basic Essentials in a backpack: pocketknife (closed, adult keeps it), rain gear, trail food, flashlight, extra clothing, first-aid kit, sun protection, map and compass, matches and fire starters (adult keeps them), water bottle and the backpack itself. Print a campground map and a campsite map, one per pair, and write the item names on index cards. Print the SAFE Checklist.
- Supplies: Essentials backpack, index cards, campground maps, a screen run by an adult (optional, for the digital-map option), SAFE Checklists, chart paper, markers, pencils
- Tell parents before they leave: The campout is [date/place] (check). Next meeting is a gear shakedown, so please send your scout with the tent, sleeping bag, pad and pack they plan to use (Req 2). Please return the permission slip and health form, parts A and B.
1. **Basic Essentials Relay** · den · 12 min · Reqs: 1
   - Say: "Cub Scouts carry six essentials. Troop Scouts carry more. Race to find each one in the pack, then tell us why you'd want it."
   - How: 1) **Two teams**. 2) The runner draws a **card**, finds the item in the pack and brings it back. Knife and matches stay in an adult's hand. 3) The runner says **one use**. 4) At the knife and the matches, stop and explain: **Knife Safety** comes before carrying a knife, and **adults carry the matches**. At camp, the den leader chooses whether an adult lights the fire or a scout lights it with an adult at their elbow.
   - Tip: **Official option:** This is the Scout Basic Essentials Backpack relay. Note each scout who named uses for the items.
2. **Find Our Campsite** · den · 12 min · Reqs: 4
   - Say: "Here's the campground. Find the road in, the parking, the restrooms, the water, and our site."
   - How: 1) Pairs get a **map**. 2) They mark the **entrance, parking, restrooms, water spigot and our site**. 3) On the site map, sketch where the **kitchen, eating area, tents and firepit** might go. 4) Optional: open the site on the **adult-run screen** (official digital-map option).
   - Tip: **Keep the sketches:** The patrol compares them with the real site on arrival for Req 6.
3. **SAFE for Our Campout** · den · 11 min · Reqs: 3
   - Say: "Four checks before any activity: Supervision, Assessment, Fitness and skill, Equipment and environment. How does each apply to our campout?"
   - How: 1) Scouts **take turns reading** the four points. 2) The patrol writes **how each applies**: which adults and the BALOO leader, weather and the firepit, health forms and swimming limits, tents and water. 3) Each scout **explains one point** in their own words.
   - Tip: **Sign off Req 3** when each scout can name all four points and one way each applies.
4. **Clean-Up & Handoff** · closing · 5 min · Reqs: none
   - Say: "Patrol leader, repack the Essentials backpack and call clean-up."
   - How: 1) **Repack** the pack. An adult keeps the knife and matches. 2) **Clean up**. 3) **Hand off** each scout, with the permission slip in the **parent's hand**.
   - Tip: **Parent in hand:** Give the slip to the adult, not the scout.

### Meeting 2 of 3 · Shakedown and Clean Kitchen · 38 min
- Option B for Req 2: the patrol goes to a troop's shakedown instead (outing). This meeting then drops step 1, and step 2 becomes 30 minutes with a longer practice.
- Prep: 1–2 weeks before, ask families to bring the gear their scout will use. Confirm an IOLS-trained adult for step 1 (check). Print a gear checklist. For step 2, bring three tubs, dish soap, a brush, tongs, a mess kit and a cooler. Use plain water only in the den room, with no bleach or hot water.
- Supplies: families' gear, gear checklists, pencils, three tubs, soap, brush, tongs, mess kit, empty cooler, pictures of foods to sort
- Tell parents before they leave: Your scout knows what to pack and how to carry it (Req 2). If the gear list shows a missing item, ask the den leader, because the pack or a troop may be able to lend one (check).
1. **Gear Shakedown** · den · 20 min · Reqs: 2
   - Say: "Lay it all out. We're checking what you'd bring overnight, including a tent and sleeping bag, and how you'll carry it."
   - How: 1) Each scout **lays out** their gear on the floor. 2) The IOLS-trained adult and the patrol **go down the checklist**. 3) Each scout says **how they'll carry it**: a pack, a duffel, or tent poles strapped on. 4) Mark **what's missing**.
   - Tip: **Cotton kills warmth:** Point out cotton sweatshirts in cold months and suggest synthetic or wool layers.
2. **Clean Camp Kitchen** · den · 13 min · Reqs: 7
   - Say: "Bad food handling makes people sick at camp. Here's how a patrol keeps its kitchen clean."
   - How: 1) **Handwashing first**, every time. 2) Pairs **sort food pictures** into "cooler" (meat, dairy, eggs, and produce above 60°F) and "box." 3) Talk about **no cross-contamination**: raw meat never shares a board or utensil. 4) Set up the **three tubs**: hot soapy wash, hot rinse, sanitizing rinse. 5) Pairs **practice with plain water** and tongs. 6) Explain that dishwater is dumped in a **designated drain or scattered 200 feet** from camp and water. 7) Each scout **explains** the steps to a leader.
   - Tip: **At camp it's hot water:** Adults pour, and scouts use tongs. Scouts show Req 7 for real at the campout.
3. **Pack-Up & Handoff** · closing · 5 min · Reqs: none
   - Say: "Everything goes home with the scout who owns it."
   - How: 1) Scouts **repack** their gear. 2) **Empty the tubs** and clean up. 3) **Hand off** each scout to their adult.
   - Tip: **Missing-gear list:** Give it to the parent, not the scout.

### Meeting 3 of 3 · The Patrol Campout (outing, overnight, about 24 hours)
- Option B (troop campout): the troop runs the schedule. The den leader asks the troop to let the AoL patrol lay out and set up its own area (Req 6), handle its own food safety and cleanup (Req 7), and hold its own debrief (Req 8) (check).
- Prep: 1–2 weeks before:
  - Confirm the BALOO-trained adult, a Hazardous Weather–trained adult and two registered adults 21 or over, and follow the council outing process (check).
  - Collect health forms (parts A and B) and review them for allergies and restrictions. Make sure another adult knows how to use any scout's EpiPen.
  - Confirm each scout's parent or guardian is coming, or make the GSS arrangement for a scout whose parent can't.
  - Plan the menu. The dinner can be the Personal Fitness meal (Option A there).
  - Pack patrol gear: dining fly, cook kit, two-burner stove, cooler, three tubs, sanitizer, hand-wash station, first-aid kit, fire bucket.
  - Check the forecast the evening before and that morning.
- Supplies: patrol gear above, campsite map and sketches from Meeting 1, health forms, trash bags, the den's SAFE chart
- Tell parents before they leave: Please do a tick check tonight. Thank you for camping with us.
1. **Arrive & Check-In** · den · 15 min · Reqs: 5
   - Say: "Everyone here? Health forms in, buddies set. Find us on the map."
   - How: 1) **Sign in** every scout and adult, and collect missing forms. 2) Scouts point to **where we are** on the Meeting 1 map. 3) Go over the **whistle signal, boundaries and buddy rule**.
   - Tip: **Weather check:** Name the lightning plan and shelter spot out loud.
2. **Site Survey** · den · 15 min · Reqs: 6
   - Say: "Before anything comes out of the car, decide where things go: kitchen, eating area, tents and firepit."
   - How: 1) The patrol **walks the site** together. 2) Check for **hazards**: dead limbs, low spots, lone trees. 3) Pick the **kitchen** (flat, near water, covered if possible), the **eating area**, the **tent spots** (flat, clear, away from branches) and the existing **firepit** with tents at a safe distance. 4) Compare with the **Meeting 1 sketches**.
   - Tip: **The patrol leader runs the discussion.** The adults check the choices.
3. **Patrol Gear First, Then Tents** · den · 45 min · Reqs: 6
   - Say: "Troop rule: patrol gear goes up before your own tent."
   - How: 1) Set up the **dining fly, kitchen table, stove area, hand-wash station and cooler spot**. 2) Only then do families **pitch their tents**. 3) Scouts **help each other**.
   - Tip: **Note Req 6** for each scout who helped set up the patrol gear before their own tent.
4. **Kitchen Open: Food Safety** · den · 20 min · Reqs: 7
   - Say: "Kitchen crew, show us how we keep food safe."
   - How: 1) Scouts run the **hand-wash station**. 2) They keep **cold food in the cooler** and the lid shut. 3) Keep **allergen-free food** separate, and prepare it first on clean surfaces. 4) An adult stands at **every stove** while dinner cooks.
   - Tip: **Personal Fitness meal:** If the patrol planned its Personal Fitness meal for tonight, scouts cook it here with adults at the stoves and all cutting done ahead of time by adults (or by scouts who have earned Knife Safety).
5. **Three-Pot Cleanup** · den · 25 min · Reqs: 7
   - Say: "Wash, rinse, sanitize, then air-dry. Show us what you learned."
   - How: 1) An adult **heats and pours** the water. 2) Scouts **scrape**, then wash, rinse and sanitize their **mess kits** with tongs. 3) Dishes **air-dry**. 4) An adult **scatters the dishwater** 200 feet from camp and water, or uses the designated drain.
   - Tip: **Note Req 7** when each scout has explained the steps and done the cleanup.
6. **Evening Program & Campfire** · den · 60 min · Reqs: 5
   - Say: "Campfire rules: sit on your seat, walk around the circle, and never throw anything in."
   - How: 1) **Adults build and tend** the fire, with a water bucket ready. 2) Scouts lead **songs and skits**. 3) Put the fire **out cold** before bed.
   - Tip: **Official page:** Review "Behavior Around Campfires" beforehand.
7. **Break Camp & Leave No Trace Sweep** · den · 90 min · Reqs: 5; 8 (set-up)
   - Say: "Leave it better than we found it. Pack out everything we packed in."
   - How: 1) **Breakfast and three-pot cleanup**. 2) **Strike tents** and pack patrol gear. 3) The patrol makes a **line sweep** of the site for trash.
   - Tip: **Tick check reminder** for every family before leaving.
8. **Start, Stop, Continue** · den · 15 min · Reqs: 8
   - Say: "Campout done. What should we start doing next time, stop doing, and keep doing? And how did we follow the Outdoor Code and Leave No Trace?"
   - How: 1) A scout **scribe** makes three columns. 2) Every scout adds **at least one idea**. 3) Each scout names **one way** the patrol followed the **Outdoor Code or Leave No Trace for Kids**. 4) Keep the list for the **next campout**.
   - Tip: **Timing:** Req 8 says "after your campout," so do this once camp is packed. If the patrol runs out of time, do it as the first step of the next den meeting and flag that meeting as an Outdoor Adventurer make-up (check).
9. **Final Headcount & Handoff** · closing · 5 min · Reqs: none
   - Say: "Buddy check! Great camping, patrol."
   - How: 1) **Count off**. 2) Make sure every scout **leaves with their adult**.
   - Tip: **Record it:** Note Reqs 5–8 on the Advancement board.

### Meeting 2 of 4 · Hurry Cases · 40 min
- Option B: skip this meeting and the next. The paramedic visit covers them.
- Prep: 1–2 weeks before, borrow **CPR manikins** (from the guest, the Red Cross or a troop, check) or use rolled blankets. Bring one breathing barrier per scout (official supply list). Write poisoning scenario cards (a strange mushroom, a spilled cleaning product, a medicine overdose, fumes). Ask the building contact where the AED is, if there is one (check).
- Supplies: manikins or rolled blankets, breathing barriers, scenario cards, FAST poster, gloves
- Tell parents before they leave: Ask your scout to teach you the FAST signs of a stroke.
1. **Heart Attack, Cardiac Arrest & Stopped Breathing** · den · 15 min · Reqs: 3
   - Say: "If someone collapses and isn't breathing normally, their heart may have stopped. Call 911, send for an AED, and start pushing hard and fast on the chest."
   - How: 1) The guest explains the difference: a **heart attack** is chest pain in a person who is awake, so call 911 and keep them calm and resting. **Cardiac arrest** means the person is unresponsive and not breathing normally. 2) The guest **demonstrates CPR** on a manikin: 30 compressions at 100–120 a minute, at least 2 inches deep, then tilt the head, lift the chin and give **2 pretend breaths** with a barrier (official activity). 3) Pairs **practice**, swapping every 30 compressions. 4) **Stopped breathing** gets the same response (official activity). 5) A leader **watches each scout**.
   - Tip: **Needs a second adult.** One adult coaches each manikin. Point out the AED if the building has one.
2. **Stroke: Think FAST** · den · 7 min · Reqs: 3
   - Say: "F is face drooping, A is arm weakness, S is speech trouble, T is time to call 911."
   - How: 1) Show the **FAST poster**. 2) Pairs take turns acting out a **sign** while the buddy **names it and does Check, Call, Care**. 3) Note who has **shown it**.
   - Tip: **Note the time:** Tell scouts to remember when the signs started, because doctors ask.
3. **Poisoning** · den · 6 min · Reqs: 3
   - Say: "Something got swallowed, spilled or breathed in. Scene safe first, then call."
   - How: 1) Pairs draw a **scenario card**. 2) They show **Check, Call, Care**: get away from the fumes, call **911 or Poison Help (1-800-222-1222)**, and keep the container to show. 3) Say that you **don't make someone throw up** unless told to (check against the AoL handbook).
   - Tip: **Keep the bottle:** Poison Help will ask what it was.
4. **Help for Choking** · den · 7 min · Reqs: 4
   - Say: "Can't talk, can't cough, hands at the throat. That's choking. Here's how you help."
   - How: 1) The guest **demonstrates** on an adult volunteer: ask "Are you choking?", then **back blows and abdominal thrusts** in turn until the object comes out (official activity). 2) Pairs **practice the hand positions** only, as the official page says: **no real blows or thrusts**. 3) A leader **watches each scout**.
   - Tip: **If they can cough, let them cough.** Stand by and encourage it.
5. **Clean-Up & Handoff** · closing · 5 min · Reqs: none
   - Say: "Wipe down the manikins. Patrol leader, call clean-up."
   - How: 1) **Wipe manikins** with a disinfectant wipe (adults). 2) **Clean up**. 3) **Hand off** each scout to their adult.
   - Tip: **Return borrowed gear** the same week.

### Meeting 3 of 4 · Shock and Everyday First Aid · 37 min
- Option B: skip this meeting (see Meeting 2).
- Prep: 1–2 weeks before, set up four stations using the official Common First Aid Practices supply list:
  - Cuts: soap, water, ointment, latex-free bandages, gauze, tape
  - Burns: two bowls of cool water, sterile gauze, aloe
  - Bites and stings: tweezers, a plastic card, a fake "tick" (a sesame seed or a sticker on an orange), ointment
  - Nosebleed: a clean cloth and a bowl of cool water

  Ask families about ointment, aloe and adhesive allergies. Bring a blanket or towel for the shock station. You need an adult at each station, so ask two parents or the guest's helpers to help (check).
- Supplies: station supplies above, station signs, a blanket, latex-free gloves, towels
- Tell parents before they leave: Ask your scout how to stop a nosebleed. Next meeting we build personal first-aid kits.
1. **Treat for Shock** · den · 8 min · Reqs: 5
   - Say: "Any serious injury can lead to shock. The signs are pale, clammy, fast breathing and confused. Here's what you do."
   - How: 1) The guest **demonstrates** with a volunteer lying down: call 911, raise the feet a little (**not** if the head, neck, back, hips or legs might be hurt), roll them on their side if they're unconscious and have no head, neck or back injury, cover with a blanket only if it's cool, give **nothing to eat or drink**, and **stay with them**. 2) Pairs **practice**. 3) A leader **watches each scout**.
   - Tip: **Volunteers only** lie down. No one is made to play the patient.
2. **Everyday First-Aid Stations** · den · 24 min · Reqs: 6
   - Say: "Four stations, six minutes each. Show the fix on your buddy."
   - How: Buddy pairs **rotate**, and an adult at each station **watches each scout**:
      - **Cuts and scratches:** wash hands, clean the wound with soap and water, apply ointment if no allergy, and cover it. For a big cut, use pressure, a gauze pad and tape, and get an adult.
      - **Burns and scalds:** cool the burn in cool water, then cover it with a clean, dry dressing. Aloe is for sunburn.
      - **Bites and stings:** pull a tick straight out with tweezers held close to the skin, scrape a stinger out with a card edge, then wash and add ointment. Tell an adult about **any animal bite**.
      - **Nosebleed:** sit up, lean **forward**, pinch the soft part of the nose for 10 minutes, and put a cool cloth above the pinch. Get help if it's still bleeding after 15 minutes.
   - Tip: **Trouble breathing after a sting** or a swelling face is an emergency: call 911 and get an adult right away (check the wording against the AoL handbook).
3. **Clean-Up & Handoff** · closing · 5 min · Reqs: none
   - Say: "Station captains, pack your station."
   - How: 1) **Repack** supplies. 2) **Wash hands**. 3) **Hand off** each scout to their adult.
   - Tip: **Restock list:** Note what ran low for Meeting 4.
