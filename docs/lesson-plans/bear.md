# Bear: required adventures (DRAFT 1, 40-minute den time)

> **A guide, not the rulebook.** These are ideas to help a den leader run the meeting. They are not a complete or official source. The official requirements on scouting.org and the Guide to Safe Scouting always come first. If something here is unclear or unsettled, use your judgment and check with the Cubmaster.

Researched 2026-09-29 by the cubmaster-program agent, using the format of Wolf draft 3. Every plan here is in our own words. Requirements are only summarized, so use the official page for the full text. Anything marked "(check)" still needs confirming. Nothing is in the app yet.

How these plans fit a Pack 569 den night:
- The pack does the opening ceremony together: flag, Pledge, Oath and Law. Each den then splits off for about 40 minutes.
- Den meetings here have no gathering or opening steps. Each one is den steps plus one short den closing. The minutes add up to 35–40 and never go over 40, so the timer can use them as they are.
- If the pack opening already covers a requirement, the meeting doesn't get a step for it. Instead it has a "Done at the pack opening:" line, and a leader confirms each scout took part.
- Outings (the Bear Habitat walk) aren't normal den nights. They keep their own length and are marked "(outing, about N min)".
- "Reqs: N" means the step does real work on that requirement. "Reqs: N (set-up)" means the step only sets up or reinforces it and does not complete it. Some outing steps use a semicolon to list both kinds, for example "Reqs: 6; 4, 8 (set-up)".
- The guides don't tick requirements. Leaders record progress on the app's Advancement board afterward.
- Bears are in 3rd grade, age 8–9. Every den room needs two registered adults 21 or over, and there is no one-on-one contact. If any girl attends, one of the two registered adults is a woman 21 or over.
- On 2026-09-29 I read all six official Bear adventure pages on scouting.org and the activity pages linked below. Plain WebFetch truncates scouting.org, so I used the r.jina.ai reader. I cross-checked everything against the Michigan Crossroads Council's Oct 2024 Bear requirements PDF, and the list matches the app's `ADVENTURES['Bear'].required`.

## Bobcat (Bear)
- Character & Leadership · Meetings: 2 · Official page: https://www.scouting.org/cub-scout-adventures/bobcat-bear/ · Other sources: https://michiganscouting.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Bear-Adventure-Requirements.pdf, https://scoutermom.com/45163/bear-bobcat-adventure/, https://www.scouting.org/cub-scout-activities/a-bear-denner/ · Checked: 2026-09-29
- Summary: Every Bear earns Bobcat again at this rank, including returning scouts. Two things are new at Bear: the three points of the Scout Oath and the denner job. Scouts say the Oath and Law at the pack opening.
- Requirements (own words):
  1. Get to know the scouts in your den. (meeting)
  2. Say the Scout Oath and Law with den and den leader. (pack opening, if the den stands together with its den leader)
  3. Learn the Scout Oath and name its three points. (meeting)
  4. Make a den Code of Conduct together. (meeting)
  5. Learn what a denner is and what a denner does. (meeting)
  6. Show the sign, salute and handshake, and when each is used. (meeting)
  7. Share a time you did your best, and why it matters. (meeting or at home)
  8. Do the Parent's Guide youth-protection activities with a parent. (at home)
- Safety notes:
  - Req 8 is for families only. Leaders send the link and never run it themselves.
  - Two registered adults in the den room. No one-on-one. If any girl attends, one of the two registered adults is a woman 21 or over.
  - The denner is a helper and is never put in charge of other scouts (official denner activity page).

### Meeting 1 of 2 · Bear Den Kickoff · 40 min
- Done at the pack opening: Req 2. The den stands together with its den leader, and a leader confirms each scout took part.
- Prep: 1–2 weeks before, email families the Parent's Guide link for Req 8. Make an Oath poster with the three points marked in three colors, and check the wording against the Bear handbook (check). Write 9–12 charades cards, each showing a simple action (see step 2). Confirm your second registered adult. On the night, set out three cones before the pack opening.
- Supplies: name tags, markers, three-color Oath poster, charades cards, 3 cones, three signs reading "Sign," "Salute" and "Handshake"
- Tell parents before they leave: Please do the Parent's Guide activities with your scout at home (Req 8), and tell the den leader when you're done. Next meeting, each scout shares a time they did their best, so help them pick one this week.
1. **Silent Line-Up** · den · 8 min · Reqs: 1
   - Say: "No talking! Line up by birthday month, January at this end and December at that end. Hand signals only."
   - How: 1) Scouts **line up silently**. 2) Check the order **out loud**: each scout says their name and birthday month. 3) In round two, line up by **how many letters are in your first name**. 4) To finish, each scout tells **one thing they did this summer**.
   - Tip: **New Bears?** Pair each new scout with a returning buddy for round two.
2. **Three Points of the Oath** · den · 15 min · Reqs: 3
   - Say: "You just said the Scout Oath at the opening. It hides three big promises: duty to God and country, duty to others, and duty to self. Let's find them."
   - How: 1) **Read the Oath** off the poster together. 2) Point to each **colored section** and name its point. 3) Play **charades**: pairs draw a card (saluting the flag, carrying a neighbor's groceries, brushing teeth, helping a younger kid), act it out, and the den guesses the point. 4) Before moving on, each scout **names all three points**.
   - Tip: **Duty to God:** Name it and move on. Each family explains what it means at home, so leaders don't teach it.
3. **Sign, Salute & Handshake Relay** · den · 12 min · Reqs: 6
   - Say: "Three cones, three moves. Run to each cone, do the move, and tell your team when a Cub Scout uses it."
   - How: 1) Give a **quick refresher**. The sign is the right arm up with two fingers raised. The salute is the same two fingers touching the eyebrow or cap brim. The handshake is the right hand with two fingers along the other scout's wrist. (check the hand details against the Bear handbook, as with Wolf) 2) Split into **two teams**. 3) At each cone the runner **does the move and says its use**: the sign for the Oath or to ask for quiet, the salute for the flag, the handshake to greet a Cub Scout. 4) **Tag** the next runner.
   - Tip: **Sign off as you go:** A scout who does all three moves and says each use has completed Req 6.
4. **Clean-Up & Handshake Line** · closing · 5 min · Reqs: none
   - Say: "Great first night, Bears. Let's leave this room better than we found it."
   - How: 1) **Two-minute clean-up**: every scout picks up five things. 2) Scouts go out through a **handshake line** past the leaders. 3) Hand each scout to their **adult** by name.
   - Tip: **Pickup:** Don't let a scout leave until you've seen their adult.
   - (at home) Req 8: families do the Parent's Guide activities.

### Meeting 2 of 2 · Our Code, Our Denner · 40 min
- Done at the pack opening: Req 2 for any scout who missed it at Meeting 1. A leader confirms each of those scouts took part.
- Prep: 1–2 weeks before, write "Bear Den Code" at the top of a poster board. Label one craft stick per scout and find a jar. Ask whether the pack has a denner cord (check). Print three-box comic strip sheets.
- Supplies: poster board, markers, chart paper, craft sticks with names, jar, denner cord (if the pack has one), comic strip sheets, pencils, crayons
- Tell parents before they leave: Our den's Code of Conduct comes to every meeting. [Name] is denner next time and will help set up, so please arrive 5 minutes early. Early set-up starts only when both registered adults are there; the scout's parent stays until then. If you haven't finished the Parent's Guide activities (Req 8), please do them this week.
1. **Write Our Den Code** · den · 12 min · Reqs: 4
   - Say: "Every good team agrees on how it treats each other. Let's write our rules, and say what we *will* do."
   - How: 1) **Brainstorm** while a leader writes everything down. 2) Turn each "don't" into a **"do,"** so "no interrupting" becomes "We take turns talking." 3) **Vote** on 4–5 rules. 4) Every scout **signs** the poster.
   - Tip: **Link to the Law:** Ask which Scout Law point each rule matches.
2. **Denner Job Chart** · den · 10 min · Reqs: 5
   - Say: "A denner is the den leader's helper for one meeting. The denner doesn't boss anyone around. They lead by example. What jobs could a denner do?"
   - How: 1) **Brainstorm jobs**, such as setting up, helping with a flag ceremony, passing out supplies, greeting scouts and leading clean-up. 2) Make a **job chart** on chart paper. 3) Show the **denner cord**: it's yellow and worn over the left shoulder. 4) **Draw a stick** from the jar to pick the next denner.
   - Tip: **Everyone gets a turn:** Set drawn sticks aside so every scout serves once before anyone repeats.
3. **Do Your Best Comic** · den · 13 min · Reqs: 7
   - Say: "Draw a three-box comic about a time you did your best: what you tried, the hard part, and what happened. Then tell us why doing your best matters."
   - How: 1) Hand out **comic sheets**. 2) Scouts **draw for 8 minutes**. 3) Volunteers **share** and each says **one sentence** on why doing your best matters. 4) Note who **shared**.
   - Tip: **Shy scout?** They can take the comic home and share it with family, because Req 7 allows that.
   - (at home) Req 7 can be finished with family.
4. **Denner-Led Clean-Up & Handoff** · closing · 5 min · Reqs: none
   - Say: "Next meeting's denner, you're in charge of the clean-up job tonight. Everyone else, follow their lead."
   - How: 1) The next denner **calls the clean-up**. 2) **Read the Code** aloud together. 3) **Hand off** each scout to their adult.
   - Tip: **Keep it visible:** Roll up the Code poster and pack it with the den box.

## Paws for Action (Bear)
- Citizenship · Meetings: 3 · Official page: https://www.scouting.org/cub-scout-adventures/paws-for-action/ · Other sources: https://michiganscouting.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Bear-Adventure-Requirements.pdf, https://scoutermom.com/19221/bear-paws-for-action-adventure-cub-scout/, https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/4/6, https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/4/7, https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/36/110 · Checked: 2026-09-29
- Summary: Scouts learn flag skills, make a craft of an American symbol, find out how a nonprofit works, and serve. All four requirements are required.
- Requirements (own words):
  1. Learn flag history, raising, lowering, folding, display and etiquette. (meeting)
  2. Name three U.S. symbols, then make a craft of your favorite. (meeting)
  3. Learn a nonprofit's mission, how it's funded, and how volunteers help. (meeting)
  4. Take part in a service project. (meeting or outing)
- Safety notes:
  - The official page points to the Service Project Planning Checklist (https://filestore.scouting.org/filestore/healthsafety/pdf/680-027.pdf) and SAFE Project Tool Use (https://filestore.scouting.org/filestore/healthsafety/pdf/680-028.pdf). Anyone using tools gets training first, and an adult supervises the whole time.
  - Off-site service or a nonprofit visit needs two registered adults, parent permission, transport planning and the council's outing process (check). If any girl attends, one of the two registered adults is a woman 21 or over.
  - Tools on an off-site service project follow the SAFE Project Tool Use chart (680-028): Bears may use rakes, shovels and trowels; wheelbarrows, handsaws, electric screwdrivers and palm sanders are Scouts BSA only, so adults use them.
  - Any online research follows digital safety, with an adult running the screen.
  - At the flagpole, an adult handles the rope cleat. Watch fingers, and no one climbs the pole.
  - Play-Doh contains wheat (check the label). Use air-dry clay if a scout has a wheat allergy.
  - Guest speakers are never alone with scouts. Both registered adults stay in the room.

### Meeting 1 of 3 · Our Flag, Up and Down · 40 min
- Prep: 1–2 weeks before, confirm there's an outdoor flagpole at the meeting site you may use, and find someone who knows its rope and clips (check). Check the sunset time: the flag is normally flown only between sunrise and sunset. If the meeting ends after dark, move step 2 to a daytime pack event. Find a spare U.S. flag for folding, because the pack's flag stays posted from the opening. Print the history cards, folding steps and trivia statements. Recruit an adult to help with folding.
- Supplies: spare U.S. flag, large towel for practice, printed folding steps, 5 history cards, true/false trivia list
- Tell parents before they leave: Next meeting your scout makes a craft of an American symbol, and we'll have a guest from a nonprofit. At home, ask your scout to show you how to fold a flag with a towel.
1. **Flag Timeline** · den · 6 min · Reqs: 1
   - Say: "Our flag has a story. Put these cards in order, oldest first."
   - How: 1) Hand out the **five cards**: the Stars and Stripes adopted on June 14, 1777 (now Flag Day); 13 stripes for the first 13 states; a new star added on the July 4 after each state joins; 50 stars today. 2) Scouts **line up** holding the cards in order. 3) Each card-holder **reads theirs** aloud.
   - Tip: **Ask why:** "Why 13 stripes but 50 stars?" makes the history stick.
2. **Raise It, Lower It** · den · 12 min · Reqs: 1
   - Say: "We raise the flag quickly and proudly, and we lower it slowly and carefully. It never touches the ground."
   - How: 1) At the pole, two scouts **hold the flag** off the ground while an adult shows the clips. 2) One scout **raises it briskly** while the others salute. 3) Show **half-staff**: raise it all the way to the top first, then bring it down to the middle. 4) **Lower it slowly** while two scouts gather it into their arms. 5) Rotate jobs so every scout **raises or lowers once**.
   - Tip: **After dark?** Skip this step tonight. Do it at a daytime pack event and record Req 1 there.
3. **Triangle Fold** · den · 12 min · Reqs: 1
   - Say: "Folded right, the flag ends up as a triangle with only the stars showing. Partners, let's try it."
   - How: 1) Pairs **practice on the towel** using the printed steps. 2) Fold the **real flag** with an adult on one end. 3) Every scout **handles the flag** once.
   - Tip: **Needs a second adult.** One adult folds with each pair while the other keeps the rest practicing.
4. **Flag Etiquette Trivia** · den · 5 min · Reqs: 1
   - Say: "Stand up if it's true, sit down if it's false!"
   - How: 1) Read statements such as "On a wall, the stars go in the upper left as you look at it" (true), "The flag can fly all night without a light" (false), "The flag may touch the ground if you're careful" (false), and "We salute when the flag passes" (true). 2) Explain **each answer** in one sentence.
   - Tip: **Sign off Req 1** once each scout has raised or lowered, folded, and answered trivia.
5. **Clean-Up & Handoff** · closing · 5 min · Reqs: none
   - Say: "You can take care of our flag now. Let's take care of this room too."
   - How: 1) **Put away** the folded flag. 2) Do a **two-minute clean-up**. 3) **Hand off** each scout to their adult.
   - Tip: **Carry it right:** A folded flag is carried with respect, not tossed in a bin.

### Meeting 2 of 3 · Symbols and Helpers · 40 min
- Prep: 1–2 weeks before, invite a guest from a local nonprofit, ideally the one the den will serve in Meeting 3. Ask for an 8-minute talk covering three things: what the group does, where its money comes from, and what volunteers do. Print symbol picture cards (flag, bald eagle, Statue of Liberty, Liberty Bell, White House, Great Seal) and prepare the craft trays.
- Supplies: symbol picture cards, air-dry clay or Play-Doh, paper, crayons, markers, small blocks or boxes, tape, question cards for the guest
- Tell parents before they leave: Crafts may need to dry, so they come home next time. Next meeting is our service project [in the meeting / off-site at (place), with a permission slip].
1. **Symbol Spotting** · den · 6 min · Reqs: 2
   - Say: "These pictures all stand for the United States. Name three, and tell us what one of them means."
   - How: 1) **Show each card** and ask the den to name it. 2) Each scout **names three symbols**. 3) Each scout **picks a favorite** to build.
   - Tip: **Keep it quick:** One fact per symbol is plenty.
2. **Make My Symbol** · den · 15 min · Reqs: 2
   - Say: "Make your favorite symbol any way you like: sculpt it, draw it or build it."
   - How: 1) Scouts choose **clay, drawing or blocks**. 2) **Build for 12 minutes**. 3) **Label** the craft with the symbol's name and your first name.
   - Tip: **Allergy check:** Use air-dry clay if any scout can't have wheat.
3. **Nonprofit Guest** · den · 14 min · Reqs: 3
   - Say: "Our guest works for a nonprofit, a group that helps people instead of making money. Listen for three things: what they do, where the money comes from, and how volunteers help."
   - How: 1) The **guest talks** for about 8 minutes. 2) Scouts ask the **three question cards**. 3) Each scout says **one thing they learned**. 4) Scouts **thank the guest**.
   - Tip: **No guest?** An adult shows the nonprofit's website on one screen and the den answers the same three questions together.
4. **Symbol Share & Clean-Up** · closing · 5 min · Reqs: none
   - Say: "Hold up your symbol and tell us its name in five words or less!"
   - How: 1) Do a **quick show-and-tell**. 2) Set crafts **flat to dry**. 3) Clean up and **hand off** to adults.
   - Tip: **Drying:** Label a tray with the den name so crafts aren't thrown out.

### Meeting 3 of 3 · Service in Action · 40 min
- Prep: 1–2 weeks before, confirm the project.
  - In the meeting (this plan): holiday or thank-you cards for military service members (an official activity option), sent through an organization that collects them. Confirm the organization and its deadline (check).
  - Off-site option: a council Scouting for Food event (see wolf.md), a park cleanup, or tree planting. Complete the Service Project Planning Checklist. Collect permission slips and health forms, plan transport, confirm two registered adults, and follow the council's outing process (check). The off-site version is an outing, not a den night. Change the header to "(outing, about 60 min)". Add a 10-minute arrival check-in and headcount, make Do the Project 35 minutes, and add tool and cleanup rules to the briefing.
- Supplies: cardstock, markers, stickers, envelopes. Off-site also needs gloves, trash bags, hand sanitizer and a first-aid kit, plus tools only if the site provides training.
- Tell parents before they leave: Thank you for helping us serve. Adults will send the cards this week. [If off-site: pickup is at the site at (time).]
1. **Job Briefing** · den · 4 min · Reqs: 4 (set-up)
   - Say: "Last time we learned how nonprofits help. Tonight we're the volunteers. Here's our job and who it's for."
   - How: 1) Explain **what we're making** and **who gets it**. 2) Assign **buddies**. 3) Show **one finished example**.
   - Tip: **Off-site:** Add the rules here: gloves on, adults handle glass and sharps, no tools without training, and stay away from the road.
2. **Do the Project** · den · 25 min · Reqs: 4
   - Say: "Let's see how much good our den can do in 25 minutes!"
   - How: 1) Buddies make **cards**. 2) Write a **short thank-you message** in each. 3) Sign with **first names only**. 4) Take a **photo of the cards**. Photograph only scouts whose parents gave photo permission; post only on the pack's official channel, with no names.
   - Tip: **Losing steam?** Set a mini-goal like "Two more cards each."
3. **Reflection Circle** · den · 6 min · Reqs: 4
   - Say: "Who are we helping, and why do volunteers matter to groups like the one we learned about?"
   - How: 1) **Circle up**. 2) Each scout gives **one sentence**. 3) Note who **took part**.
   - Tip: **Prompt:** "Because we did this, ______ will feel ______."
4. **Den Cheer & Clean-Up** · closing · 5 min · Reqs: none
   - Say: "Loudest den cheer for the people we helped, then let's clean up!"
   - How: 1) Do the **den cheer**. 2) **Collect** the cards. 3) Clean up and **hand off** to adults.
   - Tip: **Off-site:** Scouts thank the host, everyone uses **hand sanitizer**, and you do a **headcount** before handoff.

## Fellowship (Bear)
- Family & Reverence · Meetings: 2 · Official page: https://www.scouting.org/cub-scout-adventures/fellowship/ · Other sources: https://michiganscouting.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Bear-Adventure-Requirements.pdf, https://www.scouting.org/awards/religious-awards/ · Checked: 2026-09-29
- Summary: The official page names home with family as the main setting. Families lead the faith parts (Reqs 1, 2 and 4). The den does the act of kindness (Req 3) and gives scouts time to make the Req 1 art after their family talk. A scout can also earn this adventure by completing a religious emblem of the family's choosing.
- Requirements (own words):
  1. Talk faith traditions with a parent, name three celebrations, and make art. (at home; the art can be made in the meeting)
  2. Attend a religious service or reverence gathering with family. (at home)
  3. Do an act of kindness. (meeting or at home)
  4. With a parent, find two things another faith shares with yours. (at home)
- Safety notes:
  - Leaders do not teach or lead religious content, and they don't run Req 4. It happens at home with a parent.
  - Every family's traditions count. No scout has to share beliefs aloud.
  - Req 1 allows a food item, but food is made at home, not in the den, because of allergies.
  - Clay may contain wheat (check the label).
  - Scouts give their cards to people they already know, either themselves or through their family.

### Meeting 1 of 2 · Kindness in Action · 40 min
- Prep: 1–2 weeks before, send families a note:
  - Reqs 1, 2 and 4 are family-led at home.
  - Before Meeting 2, please talk with your scout about your family's faith traditions and three holidays or celebrations. That talk is what the art at the meeting is based on.
  - A religious emblem is another way to earn the adventure (link above).
  - Our chartered organization is a local church. Going to one of its services is one option for Req 2, never a requirement. Any faith's service or reverence gathering counts, and each family chooses its own.

  Gather blank cards, small plastic jars or cups, and paper strips.
- Supplies: blank cards, markers, stickers, small jars or cups, paper strips, pencils, kindness scenario list
- Tell parents before they leave: Your scout made a thank-you card to hand to a teacher or school helper this week, and a jar of kind words for you. Reqs 1, 2 and 4 happen at home and are led by your family. Please have the traditions talk before next meeting.
1. **Kind or Not?** · den · 6 min · Reqs: 3 (set-up)
   - Say: "The Scout Law says a Scout is Kind. I'll read a moment. Thumbs up if it's kind, thumbs down if it isn't, and tell us how to fix the thumbs-down ones."
   - How: 1) Read **6–8 short scenarios**, such as a new kid sitting alone at lunch or someone dropping their books. 2) Scouts vote **thumbs up or down**. 3) Fix each thumbs-down **together**.
   - Tip: **Keep it general:** Use made-up kids, not real classmates.
2. **Thank-You Cards for School Helpers** · den · 16 min · Reqs: 3
   - Say: "Think of someone at school who helps you, like a teacher, the bus driver or the librarian. Make them a thank-you card and hand it over yourself this week."
   - How: 1) Each scout **picks a person**. 2) **Decorate** the card front. 3) Write a **specific thank-you**, such as "Thanks for helping me with fractions." 4) Sign with **first name**.
   - Tip: **Help with spelling,** not with the message. The words should be the scout's own.
3. **Encouraging Words Jar** · den · 13 min · Reqs: 3
   - Say: "Now fill a jar with kind words for your parent or guardian. They can pull one out on a tough day."
   - How: 1) Each scout writes **five or more strips**, like "You make the best pancakes" or "Thanks for driving me." 2) **Fold** the strips into the jar. 3) **Decorate** the jar label.
   - Tip: **Different families:** Any grown-up at home counts, including a grandparent, foster parent or guardian.
4. **Leader Minute & Clean-Up** · closing · 5 min · Reqs: none
   - Say: "Kindness is doing something good for someone without being asked. This week, you have two gifts to give."
   - How: 1) Give the **leader minute**. 2) Do a **two-minute clean-up**. 3) **Hand off** to adults.
   - Tip: **Send it home safe:** Put each scout's card and jar in a bag with their name.
   - (at home) Reqs 1, 2 and 4 with family, plus giving the card and jar.

### Meeting 2 of 2 · Celebration Makers · 38 min
- Prep: 1–2 weeks before, remind families about the traditions talk. Set out craft trays before the pack opening.
- Supplies: air-dry clay, paper, crayons, markers, colored paper, glue sticks, paper plates for drying
- Tell parents before they leave: If you haven't finished Reqs 2 and 4 at home, please do them and tell the den leader. The art may need to dry flat overnight.
1. **Kindness Report** · den · 5 min · Reqs: 3
   - Say: "Thumbs up if you gave your card or your jar! Who wants to tell us what happened?"
   - How: 1) **Thumbs up or down**. 2) Two or three scouts **share**. 3) Note who **finished** Req 3.
   - Tip: **Didn't give it yet?** No shame. They give it this week and tell you next time.
2. **Celebration Craft** · den · 22 min · Reqs: 1
   - Say: "Make something that shows a holiday or celebration your family talked about at home. Sculpt it, draw it or build it."
   - How: 1) Scouts choose **clay or paper art**. 2) **Build for 18 minutes**. 3) **Label** it with the celebration's name if the scout wants to.
   - Tip: **Missed the home talk?** The scout can start the art now and finish the talk at home. Leaders don't suggest which celebration to pick.
   - (at home) The rest of Req 1 is the family talk.
3. **Gallery Walk (optional)** · den · 6 min · Reqs: none
   - Say: "If you want to, show your art and tell us one thing about it. Families celebrate different things, and that's great."
   - How: 1) Ask for **volunteers only**. 2) Everyone gives a **silent cheer**. 3) Thank every **presenter**.
   - Tip: **No one has to share beliefs.** Skipping is fine, so don't call on anyone.
4. **Respect Minute & Clean-Up** · closing · 5 min · Reqs: none
   - Say: "Reverence includes respect. Tell me one way we can show respect for beliefs that are different from ours."
   - How: 1) Take a few **answers**. 2) **Clean up**. 3) **Hand off** to adults.
   - Tip: **Art is wet?** Send it home flat on a paper plate.
   - (at home) Reqs 2 and 4 with family.

## Bear Habitat (Bear)
- Outdoors · Meetings: 3 (two den meetings plus one outing) · Official page: https://www.scouting.org/cub-scout-adventures/bear-habitat/ · Other sources: https://michiganscouting.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Bear-Adventure-Requirements.pdf, https://scoutermom.com/45173/bear-habitat-adventure/, https://www.scouting.org/cub-scout-activities/trash-timeline-game/, https://www.scouting.org/cub-scout-activities/fire-danger-matching/, https://www.scouting.org/outdoor-programs/leave-no-trace/, https://gatrees.org/fire-prevention-suppression/fire-weather/ · Checked: 2026-09-29
- Summary: The den plans a one-mile walk around the Leave No Trace for Kids principles, then takes it. Scouter Mom estimates 1–2 meetings plus the walk. There are nine requirements, so this plan uses two den meetings.
- Requirements (own words):
  1. Gather the Six Essentials and weather-right clothes for a one-mile walk. (meeting and outing)
  2. Find the walk on a map and confirm the one-mile route. (meeting)
  3. Learn about the path and the area around it. (meeting)
  4. Plan what to do with your own trash and trail trash. (meeting)
  5. Photograph or sketch five things to remember on the walk. (outing)
  6. Find out the fire danger rating where you'll walk. (outing; learned in the meeting)
  7. From a safe distance, spot six signs of animal life. (outing)
  8. Decide as a den how to be kind to other visitors. (meeting; practiced on the outing)
  9. Take the one-mile walk practicing Leave No Trace for Kids. (outing)
- Safety notes:
  - The walk is an outing: two registered adults, permission, and the council's outing process (check). If any girl attends, one of the two registered adults is a woman 21 or over.
  - The official page asks leaders to:
    - use the SAFE Checklist
    - take Hazardous Weather training (my.scouting.org)
    - watch the weather safety-moment and Six Essentials videos
    - review every scout's health form
    - use the buddy system and supervise actively
    - follow digital safety for photos
  - If a scout carries an EpiPen, at least one other adult must know how to use it (official page).
  - Postpone for lightning. Plan for Georgia heat and water.
  - Stay on the trail. Look but don't reach under logs or rocks (snakes). Know poison ivy. Do a tick check afterward.
  - Restrooms: buddies go together and an adult waits outside. There is no one-on-one.
  - Trail food: check allergies.
  - There is no fire on this walk. Req 6 is only about reading the rating.

### Meeting 1 of 3 · Map Our Walk · 37 min
- Prep: 1–2 weeks before, pick a park trail with a loop of about one mile (a local park, check). Walk it yourself first. Note the restrooms, parking, trail markers, the creek or bridge, and any poison ivy, and take photos of 6–8 features for "trail detective" cards. Print the park map. If you can, invite a park ranger or naturalist for step 2 (optional). Set the walk date and have permission slips ready.
- Supplies: printed park maps (one per pair), highlighters, trail detective photo cards, one tablet or phone run by an adult, chart paper, markers, permission slips
- Tell parents before they leave: The Bear walk is [date/place]. Please return the permission slip and health form. Next meeting we pack for the walk.
1. **Find It on the Map** · den · 12 min · Reqs: 2
   - Say: "Leave No Trace starts with Know Before You Go. Find our trailhead, trace our loop, and prove it's one mile."
   - How: 1) Pairs find the **parking lot and trailhead**. 2) **Highlight the loop**. 3) Mark the **restrooms** and a **halfway point**. 4) Check the distance with the **trail signs or map scale**, or on a map app with an adult running the screen.
   - Tip: **Sign off Req 2** when each pair can show the route and say how they know it's a mile.
2. **Know Your Path** · den · 12 min · Reqs: 3
   - Say: "Choose the Right Path means knowing where you're going. What will we see, and what lives there?"
   - How: 1) The **guest talks**, or the den flips through the **trail detective cards**. 2) For each card, ask **"What is it? Is it safe to touch?"** 3) Teach **"leaves of three, let it be"** for poison ivy. 4) Each scout names **one thing** to look for.
   - Tip: **No guest?** A leader who walked the trail is the expert tonight.
3. **Trail Manners** · den · 8 min · Reqs: 8
   - Say: "Be Kind to Other Visitors. Watch these quick skits and fix them."
   - How: 1) Two scouts act out **trail problems**: blocking a narrow path, shouting, running up to someone's dog. 2) The den **fixes each one**. 3) Write **three den rules** on chart paper, for example: step to the side to let people pass, use trail voices, and ask before petting a dog.
   - Tip: **Bring the rules:** Photograph the chart and read it at the trailhead.
4. **Walk Preview & Handoff** · closing · 5 min · Reqs: none
   - Say: "We've got our map and our manners. Next time we pack our gear."
   - How: 1) Do a **two-minute clean-up**. 2) Hand the **permission slip** to each **parent**. 3) **Hand off** each scout by name.
   - Tip: **Put the slip in the parent's hand,** not a scout's pocket.

### Meeting 2 of 3 · Pack It, Plan It · 37 min
- Prep: 1–2 weeks before, build a demo Six Essentials kit (first-aid kit, water bottle, flashlight, trail food, sun protection, whistle) and add decoys such as a video game, a heavy book and flip-flops. Print the official Trash Timeline and Fire Danger Matching cards. Make weather cards.

1. **Pack the Backpack** · den · 12 min · Reqs: 1 (set-up)
   - Say: "Two teams, two backpacks. Pack everything a Bear needs for a one-mile walk, and leave the junk behind!"
   - How: 1) Teams **race to pack** from the pile. 2) **Unpack together**: name each Essential and its use, and laugh at the decoys. 3) Teams draw a **weather card** and pick clothes from the bag. 4) Check for **closed-toe shoes**.
   - Tip: **Req 1 counts at the trailhead,** when each scout shows up with their own gear.
2. **Trash Timeline** · den · 10 min · Reqs: 4
   - Say: "How long does trash last outside? Put these cards on the timeline, then let's make our trash plan."
   - How: 1) Teams play the **five-minute timeline game**. 2) Reveal the **answers**. 3) Make the **den trash plan**: every scout packs out their own trash in a zip bag, scouts point out trail trash, and a gloved adult decides what to pick up. 4) Each scout **repeats the plan**.
   - Tip: **Never touch glass, sharps or needles.** Only adults decide on trail trash.
3. **Fire Danger Match** · den · 10 min · Reqs: 6 (set-up)
   - Say: "The fire danger rating tells us how easily a fire could start today. Match each card to its level."
   - How: 1) Lay out the **five levels** from Low to the highest. The scouting.org cards call it "Extremely High"; many signs say "Extreme." 2) Scouts **draw and place cards**. 3) Look up **today's rating** together on the Georgia Forestry Commission's fire weather page, with an adult running the screen (check). 4) Tell scouts they'll **check again on walk day**.
   - Tip: **Look for the sign:** Many parks post a fire danger sign near the entrance.
4. **Gear List & Handoff** · closing · 5 min · Reqs: none
   - Say: "Pack your own Six Essentials. If you forget something, you'll have to tell us what's missing!"
   - How: 1) Do a **two-minute clean-up**. 2) Hand each parent the **gear list** and a **zip bag**. 3) **Hand off** each scout by name.
   - Tip: **Collect slips:** Take any permission slips parents brought.
   - (at home) Scouts pack their own Six Essentials.

### Meeting 3 of 3 · The Bear Walk (outing, about 70 min)
- Prep: 1–2 weeks before, send families the trailhead address and meeting time. Collect permission slips and health forms. Confirm two registered adults. Follow the council's outing process (check). The adult in charge completes Hazardous Weather training and the SAFE Checklist. The evening before, check the forecast and the fire danger rating, then check the forecast again that morning. Postpone for lightning.
- Supplies: each scout's Six Essentials, clipboards, sketch sheets, pencils, six-signs tally sheets, map, den trail-manners rules, spare zip bags, gloves for adults, leader first-aid kit, health forms, extra water
- Tell parents before they leave: Pickup is at the trailhead at [time]. Please do a tick check tonight.
1. **Trailhead Check-In & Gear Check** · den · 10 min · Reqs: 1
   - Say: "Show me your Six Essentials and your shoes. Are you trail-ready?"
   - How: 1) **Sign in** each scout and collect any missing forms. 2) Each scout **shows** their Essentials and says one use. 3) **Check shoes and clothes** for the weather. 4) Everyone takes a **restroom stop** in buddy pairs, with an adult waiting outside.
   - Tip: **Forgot something?** Lend from the leader kit. The scout still names what was missing.
2. **Before We Step Off** · den · 5 min · Reqs: 6; 4, 8 (set-up)
   - Say: "What's the fire danger rating today, and what does it mean? Then buddies up, and let's hear our trash plan and trail rules."
   - How: 1) Scouts find the **rating** on the park sign or the leader's printout and **say what it means**. 2) **Pick buddies** and count off. 3) **Repeat** the trash plan and the three trail rules. 4) One adult leads at the **front** and one sweeps at the **back**.
   - Tip: **Needs a second adult.** The sweep adult never lets a pair fall behind.
3. **Walk, First Half** · den · 25 min · Reqs: 5, 7, 9
   - Say: "Look up, look down, look all around. Sketch or photograph five things to remember, and tally six signs of animals. Remember, we take only pictures."
   - How: 1) Hand out **clipboards**. 2) **Walk** at kid pace, stopping to look and listen. 3) Scouts **sketch or photograph**, using a parent's or leader's phone. 4) **Tally** tracks, nests, holes, droppings, chewed leaves, birds and bugs. 5) Call **"Buddy check!"** at the halfway point.
   - Tip: **Leave What You Find:** Photograph nature, not other visitors. Hands stay off wildlife.
4. **Walk, Second Half** · den · 20 min · Reqs: 5, 7, 8, 9
   - Say: "Head back, Bears. Let's show every visitor we pass how kind a den can be."
   - How: 1) **Finish the loop**. 2) **Step aside** for other visitors and use trail voices. 3) Take a **water break**. 4) Scouts **pack out** their own trash.
   - Tip: **Watch the distance:** The whole loop must be one mile. Don't cut it short.
5. **Share Circle** · den · 5 min · Reqs: 5, 7
   - Say: "Show us your five things and your six animal signs."
   - How: 1) **Circle up** at the trailhead. 2) Each scout **shows** their sketches or photos and tally. 3) Note who finished Reqs **5, 7, 8 and 9**.
   - Tip: **Short list?** Buddies can help each other remember, but each scout shows their own.
6. **Final Buddy Check & Handoff** · closing · 5 min · Reqs: none
   - Say: "Last buddy check! One mile, no trace. Great job, Bears."
   - How: 1) Do a **buddy check** count-off. 2) Make a **trash sweep** of the trailhead. 3) Hand each scout to their **adult** by name.
   - Tip: **Tick reminder:** Say it to every parent at pickup.

## Bear Strong (Bear)
- Personal Fitness · Meetings: 3 · Official page: https://www.scouting.org/cub-scout-adventures/bear-strong/ · Other sources: https://michiganscouting.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Bear-Adventure-Requirements.pdf, https://www.scouting.org/health-and-safety/ahmr/, https://www.scouting.org/health-and-safety/safety-moments/food-allergies/, https://www.scouting.org/health-and-safety/safety-moments/anaphylaxis/ · Checked: 2026-09-29
- Summary: Healthy food, being active with the den and alone, rest, and a health-form talk at home. It takes three meetings because Req 2 (30 minutes) and Req 3 (15 minutes) together won't fit into one 40-minute den time with a closing.
- Requirements (own words):
  1. Taste foods from three of the five food groups. (meeting)
  2. Be active 30 minutes with your den or someone, stretching and moving. (meeting; the official text also allows one other person)
  3. Do 15 minutes of your own cardio, strength and flexibility exercises. (meeting; the official text doesn't require the den)
  4. Do a relaxing activity for 10 minutes. (meeting; the official text doesn't require the den)
  5. Review your health form with a parent and discuss taking part. (at home)

  - Collect food allergies from health forms and families before the snack, and ask about religious or cultural food needs (official page).

### Meeting 1 of 3 · Bear Fuel, Bear Rest · 39 min

   - Tip: **Quick is fine:** Two minutes of sorting is plenty.
2. **Build-a-Bear Snack** · den · 18 min · Reqs: 1

4. **Favorite Food & Clean-Up** · closing · 5 min · Reqs: none
   - Say: "Tell us one food you'd eat again, then let's clean up."
