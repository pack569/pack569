# Webelos: required adventures (DRAFT 2, 40-minute den time)

> **A guide, not the rulebook.** These are ideas to help a den leader run the meeting. They are not a complete or official source. The official requirements on scouting.org and the Guide to Safe Scouting always come first. If something here is unclear or unsettled, use your judgment and check with the Cubmaster.

Researched 2026-09-30 by the cubmaster-program agent, using the format of Bear draft 1. Draft 2 (2026-09-30) applies the pack's answers to open questions 1, 4, 8 and 10. Every plan here is in our own words. Requirements are only summarized, so use the official page for the full text. Anything marked "(check)" still needs confirming. Nothing is in the app yet.

How these plans fit a Pack 569 den night:
- The pack does the opening ceremony together: flag, Pledge, Oath and Law. Each den then splits off for about 40 minutes.
- Den meetings here have no gathering or opening steps. Each one is den steps plus one short den closing. The minutes add up to 35–40 and never go over 40, so the timer can use them as they are.
- If the pack opening already covers a requirement, the meeting doesn't get a step for it. Instead it has a "Done at the pack opening:" line, and a leader confirms each scout took part.
- In the same way, if a pack meeting covers a requirement (My Community's elected official, at one of the pack's guest-speaker nights), the adventure has a "Done at a pack meeting:" line instead of a den step. The den leader confirms who was there.
- Where the pack left the choice to the den leader, the guide says "Leader's choice" (My Family) or gives "Option A / Option B" in a step's How line (My Safety).
- Outings (the two-mile Walkabout) aren't normal den nights. They keep their own length and are marked "(outing, about N min)".
- "Reqs: N" means the step does real work on that requirement. "Reqs: N (set-up)" means the step only sets up or reinforces it and does not complete it. Some steps use a semicolon to list both kinds, for example "Reqs: 3; 4, 6 (set-up)".
- The guides don't tick requirements. Leaders record progress on the app's Advancement board afterward.
- Webelos are in 4th grade, age 9–10. They can handle 10–15 minute activity chunks, some reading and writing, and denner and assistant denner jobs. Webelos come without an adult partner, so every den room needs two registered adults 21 or over, and there is no one-on-one contact. If any girl attends, one of the two registered adults is a woman 21 or over.
- On 2026-09-30 I read all six official Webelos adventure pages on scouting.org and the activity pages linked below. Plain WebFetch truncates scouting.org, so I used the r.jina.ai reader. I cross-checked everything against the Michigan Crossroads Council's Oct 2024 Webelos requirements PDF. The six required names match the app's `ADVENTURES['Webelos'].required` and are in category order. For draft 2 I re-read the Stronger, Faster, Higher, My Community and My Family pages, and the Cub Camp Cuisine, Culinary Creations, Our Elected Official and Safe Hazardous Material Storage activity pages.
- The 2024 Webelos program is very different from the old one. Older lists (for example, a My Safety with fire escape plans, or a Walkabout with a 3-mile hike and a cooked lunch) are pre-2024. Use the scouting.org links.

## Bobcat (Webelos)
- Character & Leadership · Meetings: 2 · Official page: https://www.scouting.org/cub-scout-adventures/bobcat-webelos/ · Other sources: https://michiganscouting.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Webelos-Adventure-Requirements.pdf, https://www.scouting.org/cub-scout-activities/denner-jobs/ · Checked: 2026-09-30
- Summary: Every Webelos earns Bobcat again at this rank, and it comes first. Scouts get to know the den, explain the three points of the Oath, learn the Law, write a den code and set up the denner jobs. Scouts say the Oath and Law at the pack opening.
- Requirements (own words):
  1. Get to know the scouts in your den. (meeting)
  2. Say Oath and Law with den; explain the Oath's three points. (saying: pack opening, if the den stands together with its den leader; three points: meeting)
  3. Learn about the Scout Law. (meeting)
  4. Make a den Code of Conduct together. (meeting)
  5. Learn the denner's role and duties. (meeting)
  6. Show the sign, salute and handshake, and when each is used. (meeting)
  7. Do the Parent's Guide youth-protection activities with a parent. (at home)
- Safety notes:
  - Req 7 is for families only. Leaders send the link and never run it themselves.
  - Two registered adults in the den room. No one-on-one. If any girl attends, one of the two registered adults is a woman 21 or over.
  - The denner is a helper who sets an example. The denner is never put in charge of other scouts (official Denner Jobs page).

### Meeting 1 of 2 · Webelos Den Kickoff · 40 min
- Done at the pack opening: the first half of Req 2, saying the Oath and Law. The den stands together with its den leader, and a leader confirms each scout took part. Req 2 is complete only after the scout also explains the three points in step 2.
- Prep: 1–2 weeks before, email families the Parent's Guide link for Req 7. Make an Oath poster with the three points marked in three colors, and check the wording against the Webelos handbook (check). Make "Find a Webelos who…" bingo sheets with 12 squares, for example "has a pet with scales," "was born outside Georgia," "plays an instrument" and "has been to a pack campout." Confirm your second registered adult.
- Supplies: name tags, pencils, bingo sheets, three-color Oath poster, sticky notes in three colors, index cards
- Tell parents before they leave: Please do the Parent's Guide activities with your scout at home (Req 7), and tell the den leader when you're done. Next meeting we write our den code and set up the denner jobs.
1. **Den Bingo** · den · 10 min · Reqs: 1
   - Say: "Find a different Webelos for each square and write their first name in it. One square per person, so you'll have to talk to everyone."
   - How: 1) Hand out **bingo sheets** and pencils. 2) Scouts **mingle for 6 minutes**. 3) Circle up. Each scout **introduces one person** they met and one fact they learned. 4) Leaders **play too**.
   - Tip: **New to the pack?** Pair a new scout with a returning buddy for the first two squares.
2. **Three Points of the Oath** · den · 12 min · Reqs: 2
   - Say: "You just said the Scout Oath at the opening. It holds three big promises: duty to God and country, duty to others, and duty to self. Tell me what each one looks like in real life."
   - How: 1) **Read the Oath** off the poster together. 2) Point to each **colored section** and name its point. 3) Each scout writes **one real-life example** each for duty to country, duty to others and duty to self on matching sticky notes and posts them. There's no sticky note for duty to God: the leader names it, and it's explained at home. 4) Pairs **explain all three points** to each other, and then each scout explains them to a leader.
   - Tip: **Duty to God:** The leader names it and moves on, with no posted examples. Each family explains what it means at home, so leaders don't teach it.
3. **Teach It Back: Sign, Salute & Handshake** · den · 13 min · Reqs: 6
   - Say: "Webelos are the older Cub Scouts, so you're going to learn these three moves well enough to teach a Tiger."
   - How: 1) Give a **quick refresher**. The sign is the right arm up with two fingers raised. The salute is the same two fingers touching the eyebrow or cap brim. The handshake is the right hand with two fingers along the other scout's wrist. (check the hand details against the Webelos handbook, as with Wolf and Bear) 2) In **pairs**, scouts plan a one-minute "lesson" on all three moves and when each is used: the sign for the Oath or to ask for quiet, the salute for the flag, the handshake to greet a Cub Scout. 3) Each pair **teaches the den**. 4) The den **copies** each move.
   - Tip: **Sign off as you go:** A scout who does all three moves and says each use has completed Req 6. "Teaching Younger Scouts" is an official activity option. Ask the Cubmaster whether the Webelos could teach the moves to the Tigers at a pack meeting (check).
4. **Clean-Up & Handshake Line** · closing · 5 min · Reqs: none
   - Say: "Good start, Webelos. Leave the room better than we found it."
   - How: 1) **Two-minute clean-up**: every scout picks up five things. 2) Scouts go out through a **handshake line** past the leaders. 3) Hand each scout to their **adult** by name.
   - Tip: **Pickup:** Don't let a scout leave until you've seen their adult.
   - (at home) Req 7: families do the Parent's Guide activities.

### Meeting 2 of 2 · Law, Code and Denner · 40 min
- Done at the pack opening: the first half of Req 2 for any scout who missed it at Meeting 1. A leader confirms each of those scouts took part. Those scouts also explain the three points to a leader tonight.
- Prep: 1–2 weeks before, make 12 Scout Law point cards. Write "Webelos Den Code" at the top of a poster board. Print a den calendar running from now through May. Ask whether the pack has denner cords (check).
- Supplies: Scout Law cards, Oath & Law poster, poster board, markers, index cards, pencils, den calendar printout, denner cord (if the pack has one)
- Tell parents before they leave: Our den's Code of Conduct comes to every meeting. The den calendar shows when your scout is denner or assistant denner, and the denner arrives 5 minutes early to help set up. Early set-up starts only when both registered adults are there; the scout's parent stays until then. If you haven't finished the Parent's Guide activities (Req 7), please do them this week.
1. **Missing Law Point** · den · 10 min · Reqs: 3
   - Say: "All twelve points of the Scout Law are on the floor. Close your eyes. I'll take one away. The first person to name the missing point also has to tell us what it means and give an example."
   - How: 1) Lay the **12 cards in order**. 2) Scouts **close their eyes** while a leader removes one card. 3) The first scout to name it gives the **meaning and an example**. 4) That scout **removes the next card**. Keep going until every point has been explained once.
   - Tip: **The requirement is to learn about the Law.** Memorizing it isn't required, so hints are fine.
2. **Write Our Den Code** · den · 12 min · Reqs: 4
   - Say: "Every good team agrees on how it treats each other. Let's write our rules, and say what we *will* do."
   - How: 1) A scout volunteer acts as **scribe**. 2) **Brainstorm** rules. 3) Turn each "don't" into a **"do,"** so "no interrupting" becomes "We take turns talking." 4) **Vote** on 5–6 rules. 5) Every scout **signs** the poster.
   - Tip: **Link to the Law:** Ask which Scout Law point each rule matches.
3. **Denner & Assistant Denner** · den · 13 min · Reqs: 5
   - Say: "The denner is the den leader's helper for a month. The assistant denner helps the denner and learns the job. Neither one bosses anyone around. What jobs should they do?"
   - How: 1) Each scout writes **three jobs** on an index card, such as setting up, leading a flag ceremony, passing out supplies, greeting scouts or leading clean-up. 2) Read the cards aloud, and the den **picks five jobs** for a poster. 3) Fill in the **den calendar** so every scout gets a turn. This month's assistant denner is next month's denner. 4) Show the **denner cord**.
   - Tip: **Doing My Community next?** Leave the first slot blank and fill it with the denner election in My Community Meeting 1. That election counts toward My Community Req 1.
4. **Read the Code & Handoff** · closing · 5 min · Reqs: none
   - Say: "This is our promise to each other. Let's read it together."
   - How: 1) **Read the Code** aloud together. 2) Do a **two-minute clean-up**. 3) **Hand off** each scout to their adult.
   - Tip: **Keep it visible:** Roll up the Code poster and pack it with the den box.

## My Community (Webelos)
- Citizenship · Meetings: 2 (plus the pack's guest-speaker night for Req 2) · Official page: https://www.scouting.org/cub-scout-adventures/my-community/ · Other sources: https://michiganscouting.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Webelos-Adventure-Requirements.pdf, https://www.scouting.org/cub-scout-activities/denner-election/, https://www.scouting.org/cub-scout-activities/snack-lections/, https://www.scouting.org/cub-scout-activities/our-elected-official/, https://www.scouting.org/cub-scout-activities/history-of-the-americans-with-disabilities-act/, https://www.scouting.org/cub-scout-activities/10000-for-the-troops-webelos/, https://www.scouting.org/programs/cub-scouts/2025-holiday-letters-for-military/, https://blog.scoutingmagazine.org/2024/07/09/scout-participation-at-political-events/ · Checked: 2026-09-30
- Summary: Scouts learn two ways of voting by electing a denner, build a timeline of a federal law showing all three branches, and serve. They speak with an elected official at one of the pack's guest-speaker nights, using questions they prepared in the den. All four requirements are required. The service plan uses the national holiday-card project, which has a ship-by date, [date] (2026: Nov 6). Moving the guest to a pack meeting frees about 22 minutes of den time, so the adventure now fits in two den meetings. Draft 1's Cheer Vote warm-up is folded into the denner election, and its Three Branches sort is folded into the timeline.
- Requirements (own words):
  1. Learn how majority and plurality voting differ. (meeting)
  2. Talk with an elected official about how they were elected. (pack meeting: The pack's guest-speaker night; questions prepared at a meeting)
  3. Make a timeline of a federal law showing all three branches. (meeting)
  4. Take part in a service project. (meeting or outing)
- Done at a pack meeting: Req 2, speaking with an elected official. The pack hosts several guest speakers during the year, and the whole pack listens. When one of those guests is an elected official, the Webelos do Req 2 there and not in den time ([date], check). Ask the Cubmaster to have the guest ready to talk about majority and plurality voting and how ties are broken, as the official Our Elected Official activity says. The Webelos sit together with their den leader and ask the questions they wrote at Meeting 2. The official activity says the guest "speaks with" scouts, so make sure each Webelos gets to ask or hear an answer (check). Right after the talk, each Webelos tells the den leader which kind of voting elected the guest and why, and the den leader confirms who was there. The official activity ends with a thank-you card. The pack sends it, and the Webelos can sign it (check). A Webelos who misses the night needs another way to speak with an elected official (check).
- Leader's choice for Req 2:
  - Option A · the pack guest-speaker night, as above.
  - Option B · the fallback, used if the guest-speaker night doesn't include an elected official before the den finishes My Community. The den leader arranges another way to talk with an elected official: an official visits a den meeting, or the den visits their office. An office visit uses the council outing process (check). The questions from Meeting 2 are used either way. Check the official page for any other allowed forms, such as a video call (check).
  - The guest-speaker dates and guests are unknown as of 2026-09-30.
- Safety notes:
  - The official page points to the Service Project Planning Checklist (https://filestore.scouting.org/filestore/healthsafety/pdf/680-027.pdf) and SAFE Project Tool Use (https://filestore.scouting.org/filestore/healthsafety/pdf/680-028.pdf).
  - At the pack guest-speaker night, the guest is never alone with scouts, and registered adults stay in the room.
  - The guest visit is about how elections work, not about politics. Scouting America doesn't endorse candidates. Don't let the visit become campaigning, and don't allow photos of scouts in uniform to be used in campaign material (Scouting Magazine policy post above).
  - Holiday cards: no glitter (official activity page). Scouts sign with their first name only.
  - Off-site service (Scouting for Food) needs two registered adults, parent permission, transport planning and the council's outing process (check). If any girl attends, one of the two registered adults is a woman 21 or over.

### Meeting 1 of 2 · Elect a Denner, Map a Law · 40 min
- Prep: 1–2 weeks before, cut ballot slips (two per scout per round) and bring a box for ballots. Make three signs: "Congress (makes laws)," "President (signs or vetoes laws)" and "Supreme Court (decides what laws mean)." Print the official ADA timeline template (https://filestore.scouting.org/filestore/cubscouts/adventure-resources/webelos/Webelos%20My%20Community%203%20History%20of%20the%20ADA%20Act%20timeline.pdf) and branch pictures (https://filestore.scouting.org/filestore/cubscouts/adventure-resources/webelos/Webelos%20My%20Community%203%20History%20of%20the%20ADA%20Act%20branch.pdf), one set per scout. Ask the Cubmaster for the date of the pack's guest-speaker night (see the "Done at a pack meeting" line). Write five event cards in your own words from the official activity:
  - 1988: the ADA is introduced in Congress (Congress)
  - 1989: the Senate passes it (Congress)
  - 1990: the House passes it and President George H.W. Bush signs it (Congress and President)
  - 1999: Supreme Court rulings narrow who the law covers (Supreme Court)
  - 2008: the ADA Amendments Act is signed, undoing those limits (President; Congress passed it)
- Supplies: ballot slips, pencils, ballot box, chart paper, markers, three branch signs, timeline templates, branch pictures, scissors, tape or glue sticks, colored pencils, five event cards
- Tell parents before they leave: Your scout helped elect our first denner tonight and followed a federal law through all three branches. Please ask your scout what "majority" and "plurality" mean. Next meeting is our service project: holiday cards for service members, mailed by the ship-by date ([date]). If your scout would rather do the timeline on a different federal law, they can do it at home and bring it in.
1. **Denner Election** · den · 15 min · Reqs: 1
   - Say: "Now for something that matters: our first denner. Nominate someone. If they accept, they give a 30-second speech. Then we vote and count it both ways."
   - How: 1) Take **nominations** until there are at least three. Each nominee **accepts or declines**. With three or more candidates, the winner may not have more than half the votes, and that is what shows the difference. If only two accept, also hold a quick secret ballot on **four den-cheer ideas** and count that one both ways. 2) Candidates give **30-second speeches**. 3) **Secret ballot**. 4) Count it by **plurality**, then check whether anyone has a **majority**. If not, run a **second round**. 5) Ask, "**Which way was faster? Which way means more people agreed?**"
   - Tip: **No hurt feelings:** Everyone serves on the den calendar. The election only decides who goes first. Georgia holds a runoff in many elections when no one gets more than half the vote, so this is the majority system in action (check).
2. **ADA Timeline** · den · 20 min · Reqs: 3
   - Say: "A federal law passes through all three branches. The Americans with Disabilities Act is a federal law that helps make sure people with disabilities aren't shut out of jobs, schools and buildings. Let's follow it through all three branches."
   - How: 1) Point to the **three signs** and name each branch's job: Congress makes laws, the President signs or vetoes them, and the Supreme Court decides what they mean. 2) Ask, "**Where have you seen this law at work?**" Ramps, wide restroom stalls and braille on elevator buttons are good answers. 3) Pairs get the five **event cards scrambled** and put them in order. 4) Each scout fills in **their own template**: the year, what happened in their own words, and the **branch picture** taped on. 5) Check that **all three branches** appear.
   - Tip: **Choosing another law is allowed.** Req 3 says to choose a federal law, and the ADA is the official example. A scout who picks another law finishes at home. Check that their timeline shows all three branches before recording it.
3. **New Denner's First Job & Handoff** · closing · 5 min · Reqs: none
   - Say: "Denner, you're up. Call the clean-up and lead us out."
   - How: 1) The new denner **calls clean-up**. 2) Timelines go home **flat in a folder**. 3) **Hand off** each scout to their adult.
   - Tip: **Update the calendar:** Write the winner in the first denner slot.

### Meeting 2 of 2 · Cards for the Troops · 40 min
- Prep: 1–2 weeks before, check the official activity page again for the current instructions (https://www.scouting.org/cub-scout-activities/10000-for-the-troops-webelos/). On 2026-09-30 it said:
  - Cards are no bigger than letter paper folded in half.
  - No glitter.
  - Open with "Dear Warrior" and write a greeting that suits people of any belief.
  - Add a personal note, for example about how your family celebrates.
  - Sign with your first name only, your town and state, and the pack number.
  - The den mails its own cards to: 10,000 for the Troops, Scouting America, 1325 West Walnut Hill Lane, Irving, TX 75015-2079.
  - Mail them by the ship-by date, [date] (2026: Nov 6).

  This meeting must happen early enough for an adult to mail the cards by then. A 2025 Scouting Magazine post shows ZIP 75038 and a 2025 date. Use the scouting.org page, which is newer.
  - Off-site option: the council's Scouting for Food event, which is an official activity option (check NEGA's date). It's an outing, not a den night. Complete the Service Project Planning Checklist. Collect permission slips and health forms, plan transport, confirm two registered adults, and follow the council's outing process (check). Change the header to "(outing, about 60 min)". Add a 10-minute check-in and headcount, do Questions for Our Pack Guest during the check-in, and make Do the Project 35 minutes.
- Supplies: 8½ × 11 construction paper and copy paper, markers, crayons, stickers, scissors, one large mailing envelope or box, index cards, pencils
- Tell parents before they leave: Thank you for helping us serve. An adult will mail the cards before the ship-by date. Your scout meets an elected official at a pack guest-speaker night on [date] (check), and that finishes Req 2, so please bring them.
1. **Job Briefing** · den · 4 min · Reqs: 4 (set-up)
   - Say: "Service members far from home at the holidays get cards from Cub Scouts all over the country. Tonight we make ours."
   - How: 1) Explain **who gets the cards**. 2) Go over the **rules**: "Dear Warrior," first name, town, state and Pack 569, no glitter, no bigger than folded letter paper. 3) Show **one finished example**.
   - Tip: **Greetings for everyone:** "Happy holidays" and "Thank you for your service" fit any belief.
2. **Holiday Cards for Service Members** · den · 22 min · Reqs: 4
   - Say: "Make each card your own. Tell them something about how your family celebrates."
   - How: 1) Scouts **fold and decorate**. 2) Write a **greeting** and a **personal note**. 3) **Sign** with first name, town, state and pack number. 4) Aim for **two cards each**.
   - Tip: **Spelling help, not wording help:** The words should be the scout's own.
3. **Questions for Our Pack Guest** · den · 5 min · Reqs: 2 (set-up)
   - Say: "At the pack's guest-speaker night, we meet someone who won an election. Write one question you'd like to ask them."
   - How: 1) Every scout writes **one question** on a card. 2) The leader makes sure someone asks, "**Were you elected by majority or plurality, and why?**" 3) **Collect the cards**. The den leader keeps them in the den box and hands them back at the guest night.
   - Tip: **Good starters from the official activity:** When were you first elected? How often is the election held? Is there a limit on how long you can serve? How many votes did you get? How many of the people who could vote actually voted?
4. **Reflection Circle** · den · 4 min · Reqs: 4
   - Say: "What makes someone a good citizen, and how did we act like good citizens tonight?"
   - How: 1) **Circle up**. 2) Each scout gives **one sentence**. 3) Note who **took part**.
   - Tip: **Tie it together:** Voting, laws and service are all part of being a citizen.
5. **Pack the Cards & Handoff** · closing · 5 min · Reqs: none
   - Say: "Denner, count the cards into the envelope. Everyone else, clean-up."
   - How: 1) **Count and pack** the cards. 2) **Clean up**. 3) **Hand off** each scout to their adult.
   - Tip: **Photo:** Photograph the stack of cards. Photograph only scouts whose parents gave photo permission; post only on the pack's official channel, with no names.

## My Family (Webelos)
- Family & Reverence · Meetings: 2 (Option A) or 1 (Option B) · Official page: https://www.scouting.org/cub-scout-adventures/my-family/ · Other sources: https://michiganscouting.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Webelos-Adventure-Requirements.pdf, https://www.scouting.org/awards/religious-awards/ · Checked: 2026-09-30
- Summary: The official page says this adventure is "commonly done at home." Reqs 1, 3 and 4 are all worded "with your parent or legal guardian," so families lead them. The den does the act of kindness (Req 2) at Meeting 1. The den leader chooses how to handle Req 1. With Option A, parents come to a craft night at Meeting 2, and Req 1 is done at the den with the parent there. With Option B, Reqs 1, 3 and 4 go home and the adventure's den time ends after Meeting 1. A scout can also earn this adventure by completing a religious emblem of the family's choosing.
- Requirements (own words):
  1. With a parent, talk faith traditions, name three celebrations, make something. (Option A: at Meeting 2 with the parent at the table, or at home; Option B: at home)
  2. Do an act of kindness. (meeting or at home)
  3. With a parent, find two things another faith shares with yours. (at home)
  4. With a parent, discuss reverence and how you practice it. (at home)
- Safety notes:
  - Leaders do not teach or lead religious content. In Option A, leaders set out supplies at Meeting 2, and parents lead the conversation.
  - Every family's traditions count. No scout has to share beliefs aloud.
  - Req 1 allows a food item, but food is made at home, not in the den, because of allergies.
  - Req 2 service: the official page points to the Service Project Planning Checklist and SAFE Project Tool Use (links under My Community). Tool training comes first, and an adult supervises the whole time.
  - No requirement here asks families to attend a service, so the chartered-organization line isn't needed (see Open questions).
- Leader's choice: Option A · Parents-invited craft night: parents come to Meeting 2, and Req 1's "with your parent or legal guardian" parts count at the den (2 den meetings). / Option B · At home: Reqs 1, 3 and 4 are done at home, and My Family is 1 den meeting (Meeting 1 only).

### Meeting 1 of 2 · Kindness Crew · 37 min
- Option B: this is the only My Family den meeting (1 of 1). Req 1 moves home along with Reqs 3 and 4.
- Prep: 1–2 weeks before, send families a note. Send only the paragraph for the option you chose:
  - Both options: Reqs 1, 3 and 4 are done with a parent or guardian. A religious emblem is another way to earn the adventure (link above).
  - Option A: Meeting 2 on [date] is Family Craft Night. If you can stay, your scout makes the Req 1 craft with you there. If you can't, finish Req 1 at home. Do Reqs 3 and 4 at home.
  - Option B: Do all of Req 1 at home. Talk about your family's faith traditions, name three holidays or celebrations, and make a craft, work of art or food item together. Then do Reqs 3 and 4. Tell the den leader when each is done. If your scout misses Meeting 1, a kind act at home that you confirm counts for Req 2.

  Ask the building contact which shared space the den may tidy for them (for example, stacking chairs, wiping tables or sorting a lost-and-found) (check). Bring cleaning cloths and a bucket of water.
- Supplies: work gloves, cloths, bucket of water, trash bags, cardstock, markers, pencils
- Tell parents before they leave: Your scout did a kind act for the people who host our meetings (Req 2). Reqs 1, 3 and 4 are done with you. Option A: Next meeting is Family Craft Night, so please stay if you can. Option B: That's the only den meeting for My Family. Please do Reqs 1, 3 and 4 at home and tell the den leader when they're done.
1. **What Counts as Kindness?** · den · 6 min · Reqs: 2 (set-up)
   - Say: "A Scout is Kind. Kindness means doing something good for someone without being asked. Give me examples at home, at school and around town."
   - How: 1) Make **three columns** on chart paper: home, school, town. 2) Scouts **fill them in**. Doing dishes, helping with a chore and helping a neighbor with yard work are all official examples. 3) Point out that the **people who host us** keep this building running for us.
   - Tip: **No money needed:** The best examples cost nothing.
2. **Leave It Better for Our Hosts** · den · 18 min · Reqs: 2
   - Say: "Tonight our den is the kindness crew for the people who let us meet here. Let's leave their space better than we found it."
   - How: 1) Assign **buddy pairs** to jobs: stack chairs, wipe tables, sort the lost-and-found, pick up litter in the entry. 2) **Gloves on** for trash. 3) Both adults stay with the den, and **no one goes off alone**. 4) Buddy pairs **switch jobs** halfway.
   - Tip: **Adults handle anything sharp, broken or heavy.** Use water only. Don't use cleaning chemicals.
3. **Notes for the Helpers** · den · 8 min · Reqs: 2

4. **Kindness Pledge & Handoff** · closing · 5 min · Reqs: none

   - How: 1) Take **quick answers**. 2) **Put away** the supplies. 3) **Hand off** each scout to their adult.
   - Tip: **Hand the family note to the adult,** not to the scout.
   - (at home) Option A: Reqs 3 and 4 with a parent, plus Req 1 if the parent can't come to Meeting 2. Option B: Reqs 1, 3 and 4 with a parent.

- Tell parents before they leave: If you couldn't stay tonight, please finish Req 1 at home with your scout by having the traditions talk, and tell the den leader. Reqs 3 and 4 are also done at home with you.

   - How: 1) **Thumbs up or down**. 2) Two or three scouts **share**. 3) Note that everyone who took part in Meeting 1 has **finished Req 2**.

## Webelos Walkabout (Webelos)
- Outdoors · Meetings: 3 (two den meetings plus one outing) · Official page: https://www.scouting.org/cub-scout-adventures/webelos-walkabout/ · Other sources: https://michiganscouting.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Webelos-Adventure-Requirements.pdf, https://www.scouting.org/cub-scout-activities/plan-a-route/, https://www.scouting.org/cub-scout-activities/safety-first/, https://www.scouting.org/cub-scout-activities/trail-first-aid/, https://www.scouting.org/health-and-safety/safe/, https://www.scouting.org/cub-scout-activities/grab-the-six-essentials/, https://www.scouting.org/outdoor-programs/leave-no-trace/, https://scoutermom.com/19396/webelos-walkabout-adventure-cub-scout/ · Checked: 2026-09-30
- Summary: Scouts plan a two-mile walk, apply the SAFE Checklist, learn trail first aid, take the walk, and then talk it over as a den. The official page doesn't require cooking. Trail food is one of the Six Essentials, and each scout brings their own. Scouter Mom suggests one meeting plus the walk, or two shorter meetings. With 40-minute den time this plan uses two meetings plus the walk.
- Requirements (own words):
  1. Get Six Essentials and weather-right clothes and shoes for two miles. (meeting and outing)
  2. Plan a two-mile route. (meeting)
  3. Check the forecast for your walk's time. (at home the evening before; confirmed at the trailhead)
  4. Review the SAFE Checklist's four points for your walk. (meeting)
  5. Show first aid for blister, sprain, sunburn, dehydration, heat illness. (meeting)
  6. Walk two miles using Leave No Trace and the Outdoor Code. (outing; the official text allows den, pack or family)
  7. Afterward, discuss with your den what went well and what to change. (outing)
- Safety notes:
  - The walk is an outing: two registered adults, permission, and the council's outing process (check). If any girl attends, one of the two registered adults is a woman 21 or over.
  - The official page asks leaders to:
    - use the SAFE Checklist
    - review the Age Appropriate Guidelines (https://filestore.scouting.org/filestore/HealthSafety/pdf/680-685.pdf)
    - take Hazardous Weather training (my.scouting.org)
    - watch the weather safety video
    - review every scout's health form (https://filestore.scouting.org/filestore/HealthSafety/pdf/680-001_AB.pdf)
    - use the buddy system and supervise actively
  - If a scout carries an EpiPen, at least one other adult must know how to use it (official page).
  - Postpone for lightning. Georgia heat is the big risk on a two-mile walk: go in the morning or in a cool month, and carry extra water.
  - Stay on the trail. Know poison ivy. Watch for bikes on greenways and cars at road crossings. Do a tick check afterward.
  - Restrooms: buddies go together and an adult waits outside. There is no one-on-one.
  - Trail food: each scout eats their own. No sharing, because of allergies. Ask families to pack nut-free food if any scout has a nut allergy.
  - First-aid practice: use latex-free bandages, and ask families about skin sensitivities before using sunscreen or aloe.
  - There is no fire or cooking on this walk.

### Meeting 1 of 3 · Plan Our Two Miles · 40 min
- Prep: 1–2 weeks before, pick one or two candidate routes of two miles, either a loop or one mile out and back. Pick local parks or greenways the den leader knows (check). Walk the route yourself first, and note the restrooms, parking, mile markers, road crossings, creeks and any poison ivy. Print maps with the scale showing, one per pair. Print the SAFE Checklist and the official Webelos Walkabout plan form (linked from the Plan a Route activity). Set the walk date and have permission slips ready.
- Supplies: maps, highlighters, Walkabout plan forms, printed SAFE Checklists, one screen run by an adult, chart paper, markers, Six Essentials lists, permission slips
- Tell parents before they leave: The Walkabout is [date/place]. Please return the permission slip and health form. The evening before, help your scout check the forecast for the walk time (Req 3).
1. **Five W's Route Plan** · den · 15 min · Reqs: 2
   - Say: "Good hikes start with five questions: Where are we going? When will we be back? Who's coming? Why are we going? What are we bringing? Let's plan exactly two miles."
   - How: 1) Pairs **highlight a route** on the map. 2) They **prove it's two miles** using the scale, mile markers or a map app run by an adult. 3) They mark the **start, halfway point, restrooms and road crossings**. 4) The den fills in the **plan form**. 5) If there are two routes, the den **votes**.
   - Tip: **Out-and-back is easiest:** Turn around at the one-mile marker.
2. **SAFE Check** · den · 10 min · Reqs: 4
   - Say: "Scouting uses four checks before any activity: Supervision, Assessment, Fitness and skill, and Equipment and environment. How does each one apply to our walk?"
   - How: 1) Scouts **take turns reading** the four points. 2) For each point, the den writes **how it applies** on chart paper:
      - Supervision: two adults, one leading and one sweeping, plus buddies.
      - Assessment: road crossings, bikes, heat, creeks.
      - Fitness and skill: health forms, water, a pace everyone can keep.
      - Equipment and environment: shoes, Six Essentials, weather.
     3) Each scout **explains one point** in their own words.
   - Tip: **Sign off Req 4** once each scout can name all four points and one way each applies.
3. **Forecast Detectives** · den · 6 min · Reqs: 3 (set-up)
   - Say: "Let's read a forecast like pros. What matters for a two-mile walk?"
   - How: 1) On the **adult-run screen**, open the hourly forecast on weather.gov. 2) Find the **temperature, rain chance, thunderstorms and heat index**. 3) Agree on **go/no-go rules**: any thunder or lightning means we postpone.
   - Tip: **The real check is the evening before,** with family. Scouts report it at the trailhead.
4. **Six Essentials List** · den · 4 min · Reqs: 1 (set-up)
   - Say: "Name the Six Essentials. What would you add for two miles in Georgia?"
   - How: 1) Name the **six**: first-aid kit, water bottle, flashlight, trail food, sun protection and whistle. 2) Add **closed-toe shoes, a hat and weather-right clothes**. 3) Each scout takes a **list home**.
   - Tip: **Water matters most:** Two miles in Georgia needs a full bottle.
5. **Walk Preview & Handoff** · closing · 5 min · Reqs: none
   - Say: "Route planned, SAFE checked. Next time we learn trail first aid."
   - How: 1) **Two-minute clean-up**. 2) Hand the **permission slip** and plan form to each **parent**. 3) **Hand off** each scout by name.
   - Tip: **Put the slip in the parent's hand,** not a scout's pocket.

### Meeting 2 of 3 · Trail First Aid · 40 min
- Prep: 1–2 weeks before, gather the official activity's supplies: moleskin, adhesive bandages (latex-free), elastic bandages, aloe gel, sunscreen, water, a sports drink, small zip bags. Write injury cards for blister, sprained ankle, sunburn, and dehydration or heat illness. You can also invite a local Scouts BSA troop's first-aid team or a first responder, which are both official options (check). Guests are never alone with scouts. Check the steps against the Webelos handbook (check).
- Supplies: first-aid supplies above, cold packs, injury cards, four station signs, small cups (no shared bottles)
- Tell parents before they leave: Ask your scout to show you how to treat a blister. Please return permission slips and health forms for the walk.
1. **Tell an Adult First** · den · 4 min · Reqs: 5 (set-up)
   - Say: "On a trail, the first step in any first aid is to tell an adult right away. Then you can help."
   - How: 1) Practice the **call**: "Adult, [name] needs help!" 2) Explain that **adults decide** whether to call 911 or end the walk.
   - Tip: **Keep it calm:** These are small problems if we catch them early.
2. **First-Aid Stations** · den · 25 min · Reqs: 5
   - Say: "Four stations, six minutes each. At every station, you show the fix on your buddy."
   - How: Buddy pairs **rotate** through the stations:
      - **Blister:** stop as soon as a spot feels hot, keep it clean and covered, and pad it with moleskin.
      - **Sprained ankle:** stop and rest, apply a cold pack, wrap it snug but not tight, and raise it.
      - **Sunburn:** prevent it with sunscreen and a hat, then treat it with shade, cool water and aloe.
      - **Dehydration and heat illness:** watch for thirst, headache, dizziness, cramps and tiredness. Move to shade, loosen clothing, cool off and sip water. Hot skin, confusion or fainting is an emergency, so call 911.
     A leader **watches each scout** show every fix.
   - Tip: **Needs a second adult.** One adult checks the blister and ankle stations while the other checks sunburn and heat.
3. **Injury Card Walk** · den · 6 min · Reqs: 5
   - Say: "We're 'on the trail' now. When I flip a card, someone has that problem, and the den has to fix it."
   - How: 1) The den **walks laps** around the room. 2) Flip an **injury card**. 3) A scout **treats it** while the den coaches. 4) Note who has **shown all four fixes**.
   - Tip: **Missed a station?** That scout does the fix here.
4. **Pack the Kit & Handoff** · closing · 5 min · Reqs: none
   - Say: "Everything goes back in the den first-aid kit. It's coming on our walk."
   - How: 1) **Repack** the supplies. 2) **Clean up**. 3) **Hand off** each scout to their adult.
   - Tip: **Kit check:** Restock anything used before walk day.

### Meeting 3 of 3 · The Two-Mile Walk (outing, about 110 min)
- Prep: 1–2 weeks before, send families the trailhead address and meeting time. Collect permission slips and health forms. Confirm two registered adults. Follow the council's outing process (check). The adult in charge completes Hazardous Weather training and the SAFE Checklist. Make sure another adult knows how to use any scout's EpiPen. The evening before, check the forecast, then check it again that morning. Postpone for lightning or dangerous heat.
- Supplies: each scout's Six Essentials, route map, the den's SAFE chart, the den first-aid kit, health forms, extra water, gloves and a trash bag for adults, a whistle
- Tell parents before they leave: Pickup is at the trailhead at [time]. Please do a tick check tonight.
1. **Trailhead Check-In & Gear Check** · den · 10 min · Reqs: 1
   - Say: "Show me your Six Essentials, your shoes and your water. Are you ready for two miles?"
   - How: 1) **Sign in** each scout and collect any missing forms. 2) Each scout **shows** their Essentials. 3) **Check shoes, clothes and water**. 4) Everyone takes a **restroom stop** in buddy pairs, with an adult waiting outside.
   - Tip: **Forgot something?** Lend from the leader kit. The scout still names what was missing.
2. **Go / No-Go** · den · 5 min · Reqs: 3; 4, 6 (set-up)
   - Say: "What did the forecast say for right now? Is it a go? Then buddies up, and remind us how we leave no trace."
   - How: 1) Each scout **reports the forecast** they checked. 2) Run through the **SAFE chart** quickly. 3) Say the four Outdoor Code promises (clean, careful with fire, considerate, conservation-minded) and the **Leave No Trace for Kids** principles. 4) **Pick buddies**. One adult leads at the **front** and one sweeps at the **back**.
   - Tip: **Needs a second adult.** The sweep adult never lets a pair fall behind.
3. **Walk, First Mile** · den · 35 min · Reqs: 6
   - Say: "Trail voices, stay on the path, and step aside for other walkers. Next stop is the one-mile mark."
   - How: 1) **Walk** at a pace everyone can keep. 2) Call **"Buddy check!"** at every road crossing. 3) Each scout **packs out** their own trash.
   - Tip: **Hot spot?** Stop and use the blister fix from Meeting 2. That's first aid for real.
4. **Halfway Break** · den · 10 min · Reqs: 6
   - Say: "Water, shade, and your own trail food. Anyone feeling a hot spot or getting too hot?"
   - How: 1) **Sit in the shade**. 2) **Drink water** and eat your **own** trail food. 3) Put **sunscreen** back on. 4) Do a **buddy check** before you turn back.
   - Tip: **No food swaps.** That rule protects scouts with allergies.
5. **Walk, Second Mile** · den · 35 min · Reqs: 6
   - Say: "Head back, Webelos. Leave the trail cleaner than we found it."
   - How: 1) **Finish the route**. 2) Point out **trail trash**, and a gloved adult decides what to pick up. 3) Take a **water break** halfway back.
   - Tip: **Watch the distance:** The full two miles is required, so don't cut it short.
6. **What Went Well?** · den · 10 min · Reqs: 7
   - Say: "Two miles done! Tell us one thing that went well and one thing you'd do differently next time."
   - How: 1) **Circle up** in the shade. 2) Each scout shares **one "went well" and one "change."** 3) A leader **writes the list** for the den's next outing. 4) Note who finished **Reqs 1, 3, 6 and 7**.
   - Tip: **Walked with family instead?** Req 6 allows it, but Req 7 is a den discussion. Do it at the next den meeting.
7. **Final Buddy Check & Handoff** · closing · 5 min · Reqs: none
   - Say: "Last buddy check! Two miles, no trace. Great job, Webelos."
   - How: 1) Do a **buddy check** count-off. 2) Make a **trash sweep** of the trailhead. 3) Hand each scout to their **adult** by name.
   - Tip: **Tick reminder:** Say it to every parent at pickup.

## Stronger, Faster, Higher (Webelos)
- Personal Fitness · Meetings: 3 · Official page: https://www.scouting.org/cub-scout-adventures/stronger-faster-higher/ · Other sources: https://michiganscouting.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Webelos-Adventure-Requirements.pdf, https://www.scouting.org/cub-scout-activities/culinary-creations/, https://www.scouting.org/cub-scout-activities/cub-camp-cuisine/, https://www.scouting.org/health-and-safety/safety-moments/food-allergies/, https://www.scouting.org/health-and-safety/safety-moments/anaphylaxis/, https://filestore.scouting.org/filestore/HealthSafety/pdf/680-001_AB.pdf · Checked: 2026-09-30
- Summary: Plan, cook and eat a balanced meal; be active with the den and alone; rest; and a health-form talk at home. The den plans the meal at Meeting 1, and the meal is cooked somewhere else: at home with each scout's family, or by the den at a pack campout or a Webelos den campout, with adults running the stoves (the pack's cooking rule). Req 1 reads "With your den or family, plan, cook, and eat a balanced meal." Its official activity options are a meal on a pack campout (Cub Camp Cuisine), a den meal (Culinary Creations) and a meal planned for a family dinner. That leaves three den nights, because Req 2 (30 minutes) and Req 3 (15 minutes) can't share a 40-minute den time. The official den and campout examples both use chef's knives, so in this plan scouts do no knife work unless they have earned Chef's Knife. Whether den planning plus family cooking counts is still open (check; see Open questions).
- Requirements (own words):
  1. Plan, cook and eat a balanced meal with den or family. (plan: meeting; cook and eat: at home with family, or at a pack campout or Webelos den campout, with adults running the stoves. The official text says "den or family," and a pack campout meal is an official option)
  2. Be active 30 minutes with others, stretching and moving. (meeting)
  3. Do 15 minutes of your own cardio, strength and flexibility exercises. (meeting)
  4. Do a relaxing activity for 10 minutes. (meeting)
  5. Review your health form with a parent and discuss taking part. (at home)
- Safety notes:
  - Scouts must complete the Chef's Knife elective before they use a knife (official page). At a pack campout, adults cut everything beforehand. At home, the family note asks an adult to do the knife work unless the scout has earned Chef's Knife.
  - Collect food allergies from health forms and families, and ask about religious or cultural food needs (official page).
  - If a scout carries an EpiPen, at least one other adult must know how to use it (official page).
  - Follow the Keep Your Food Safe guide linked from the official page: keep cold food in a cooler, wash hands, and don't let allergens touch other food.
  - At a pack campout:
    - a BALOO-trained leader sets up the food area and oversees clean-up (official Cub Camp Cuisine activity)
    - an adult stands at every stove or grill
    - use oven mitts and long tools, and set up a hand-washing station
    - know where the fire extinguisher or water bucket is
    - let stoves and grills cool before cleaning
  - At home, the family supervises the stove and oven.
  - Take water breaks during active games. Check the heat if you're outdoors.

### Meeting 1 of 3 · Menu Planners · 37 min
- Prep: 1–2 weeks before, collect allergy and diet information. Send the health-form link for Req 5. Decide where the meal gets cooked. It can be at a pack campout or a Webelos den campout, if one falls soon (check the pack calendar and that a BALOO-trained leader will be there), or at home with each scout's family. Adults run the stoves. Send families a note:
  - Your scout plans a balanced meal with the den (Req 1). Then the meal is cooked and eaten with you at home, or with the den at the pack campout on [date].
  - At home, your scout leads. Go over the den's plan together, adjust it for your kitchen and your family's tastes, then cook it together and eat it together. An adult handles knives unless your scout has earned Chef's Knife.
  - Tell the den leader when you've done it and which food groups were on the plate.
  - Please also review the health form with your scout (Req 5).

  Print food group cards and a one-page menu planner, one per scout so each scout can take a copy home. Ask each scout to bring a book for step 3, and bring calm music and a speaker.
- Supplies: food group cards, menu planners, pencils, chart paper, books, calm music and a speaker, timer
- Tell parents before they leave: Please cook and eat the den's meal with your scout at home (Req 1), or we'll cook it together at the pack campout on [date]. Tell the den leader when it's done. Please review your scout's health form with them at home (Req 5). Next meeting is very active, so send sneakers and a water bottle. Tell us about any food allergy we don't already know.
1. **Balanced Plate** · den · 6 min · Reqs: 1 (set-up)
   - Say: "A balanced meal has foods from the five groups: fruits, vegetables, grains, protein and dairy. Sort these cards and tell me which group is easy to forget."
   - How: 1) Pairs **sort the cards** into five groups. 2) Ask, "**What would half your plate be?**" The answer is fruits and vegetables. 3) Show that **one dish can cover several groups**.
   - Tip: **Keep it quick:** This is a warm-up for the planning.
2. **Plan Our Meal** · den · 14 min · Reqs: 1
   - Say: "You're the chefs. Plan a meal that uses all five food groups, that you can cook without any knife work, and that's safe for everyone who'll eat it."
   - How: 1) Give the **rules**: every group included, no knife work by scouts, and safe for every scout's allergies. 2) Offer a **starter idea**: quesadillas or foil-packet fajitas (tortillas, cheese, beans or pre-cooked chicken strips, peppers an adult has cut) with fruit, baby carrots and milk. 3) The den **changes it or picks another meal** that meets the rules. 4) **At a pack campout:** write the **shopping list** and **job list**: hand-washer checker, builders, cooks (with an adult at each stove or grill), table setters, clean-up crew. **At home:** each scout copies the menu and shopping list onto **their own planner** to take home.
   - Tip: **Allergy swaps:** Plan corn tortillas for wheat allergies and dairy-free cheese for milk allergies. Cook those first on a clean surface.
3. **Ten-Minute Unwind** · den · 12 min · Reqs: 4
   - Say: "Strong bodies need rest too. For ten minutes, read your book or lie back and listen to the music."
   - How: 1) **Dim the lights** if you can. 2) Scouts choose **reading or music**. 3) Run the **timer for 10 minutes**. 4) Ask, "**How does your body feel now?**"
   - Tip: **Wiggly den?** Lower your voice, and they'll follow.
4. **Menus Home & Handoff** · closing · 5 min · Reqs: none
   - Say: "Denner, read out our menu. Campout cooks, here are your jobs."
   - How: 1) The denner **reads the menu**, and the job list if the den is cooking at a pack campout. 2) **Clean up**. 3) **Hand off** each scout to their adult, with the menu planner in the **parent's hand**.
   - Tip: **Leader homework (campout):** Shop from the scouts' list with the campout's food lead. Cut the vegetables and fruit ahead of time and keep them cold.
   - (at home) Req 1: cook and eat the den's meal with family. Or, at a pack campout: the den cooks and eats it together.
   - (at home) Req 5: review the health form with a parent.

### Meeting 2 of 3 · Game On · 35 min
- Prep: 1–2 weeks before, book a gym or field and remind families to send sneakers and water. Check the heat index and daylight if you're outdoors.
- Supplies: soft flying disc, soccer ball, cones, pinnies or bandanas, water. Indoors, use a beach ball or trainer volleyball and a net or a rope.
- Tell parents before they leave: Next meeting your scout builds their own workout. If you haven't cooked the den's meal with your scout yet (Req 1) or reviewed the health form (Req 5), please do them this week and tell the den leader.
1. **Warm-Up Stretch** · den · 5 min · Reqs: 2
   - Say: "Warm muscles work better. Stretch high, low and side to side. Get water whenever you need it."
   - How: 1) Do **arm circles**, a **toe reach** and a **side bend**. 2) Do **leg swings** holding a buddy's shoulder. 3) Point out the **boundaries**.
   - Tip: **Start the clock:** Req 2's 30 minutes start here and run through the cool-down.
2. **Ultimate Disc** · den · 12 min · Reqs: 2
   - Say: "No running with the disc. Pass to teammates and catch it in the end zone to score. No contact."
   - How: 1) Make **two even teams** with pinnies. 2) **Cone off** the end zones. 3) Everyone on a team must **touch the disc** before a score counts. 4) Take a **water break** halfway.
   - Tip: **Official option:** Ultimate, soccer and volleyball are all official activity options for Req 2.
3. **Small-Sided Soccer** · den · 8 min · Reqs: 2
   - Say: "Small goals, small teams, lots of touches. Pass before you shoot!"
   - How: 1) Set up **cone goals**. 2) Play **no goalies**. 3) **Switch teams** once.
   - Tip: **Indoors?** Swap in volleyball with a beach ball.
4. **Cool-Down Stretch** · den · 5 min · Reqs: 2
   - Say: "Slow walk, deep breaths, and stretch it out."
   - How: 1) Take a **slow lap**. 2) Repeat the **warm-up stretches**. 3) Take a **water break**.
   - Tip: **Note Req 2** now. That's 30 active minutes together.
5. **Stack the Cones & Handoff** · closing · 5 min · Reqs: none
   - Say: "Which game got your heart pumping most?"
   - How: 1) Take **quick answers**. 2) **Stack the cones** and collect the balls. 3) **Hand off** each scout to their adult.
   - Tip: **Water bottles:** Check the water line so no bottle gets left behind.

### Meeting 3 of 3 · My Webelos Workout · 38 min
- Prep: 1–2 weeks before, print move cards in three colors:
  - Cardio: jumping jacks, high knees, jog in place, skater hops
  - Strength: squats, wall sit, push-ups, plank
  - Flexibility: toe reach, butterfly stretch, lunge stretch, cat-cow

  Bring mats or towels.
- Supplies: move cards, workout sheets, pencils, timer, mats or towels, water
- Tell parents before they leave: Ask your scout to teach you their workout. If you haven't cooked the den's meal with your scout yet (Req 1) or reviewed the health form (Req 5), please do them this week and tell the den leader.
1. **Heartbeat Detective** · den · 5 min · Reqs: 3 (set-up)
   - Say: "Find your pulse and count it for 15 seconds. Then 30 seconds of jumping jacks, and count again."
   - How: 1) **Count at rest**. 2) Do **30 seconds of jumping jacks**. 3) **Count again** and compare. 4) Explain that **cardio** makes the heart work harder.
   - Tip: **Can't find it?** The side of the neck is easier than the wrist.

### Meeting 1 of 2 · Hazard Hunters · 40 min
- Prep: 1–2 weeks before, email families the official parent notification, the video link (Req 1) and a note that scouts will look for hazards at home with a parent before Meeting 2 (Req 2). Get the products ready for the option you chose:
  - Option A · photo cards: make 10–12 photo cards of real product labels (drain cleaner, bleach, oven cleaner, motor oil, lighter fluid, paint thinner, bug spray, medicine). Circle the warning symbol and the first-aid section on each.
  - Option B · real products: bring 8–10 sealed, unopened products in their original containers, from the same list or the official activity's list (drain cleaner, motor oil, furniture polish, oven cleaner, cleaning products, paint). Leave out anything leaking and anything that can be sprayed, such as aerosol cans and trigger sprays (check). Mark the warning symbol and first-aid section with removable sticky flags. Keep them in a closed bin, not in the room's cabinets.

  Print the meeting-place checklist (https://filestore.scouting.org/filestore/pdf/mpinsp.pdf) and the official Safe Space Checklist (https://filestore.scouting.org/filestore/cubscouts/adventure-resources/webelos/Webelos%20My%20Safety%203%20Safe%20Space%20Checklist.pdf). Tell the building contact the den will walk the halls (check).
- Supplies: label photo cards (Option A) or sealed products in a closed bin (Option B), three storage signs ("locked or high cabinet," "garage shelf away from heat," "never here: under the sink with no lock or in a drink bottle"), clipboards, checklists, pencils, Hazard Labels sticker sheets (optional, from the official activity)
- Tell parents before they leave: Please watch the Protect Yourself Rules video with your scout (Req 1). 1. **Symbol Spotting** · den · 10 min · Reqs: 2 (set-up)
   - Say: "Dangerous products wear warning signs. Learn to read them, and you'll know what to do if someone gets exposed."
   - How: 1) Show the **symbols**: flame (flammable), skull and crossbones (poison), and a hand being eaten away (corrosive). 2) Explain the **signal words**: Caution, Warning, Danger. 3) **Option A · photo cards:** pairs find the **"first aid" or "if swallowed" section** on their photo card. / **Option B · real products:** an adult sets one **sealed product** in front of each pair. Pairs turn it to find the **"first aid" or "if swallowed" section**, and **no one opens** a container. 4) Everyone repeats the **Poison Help number**: 1-800-222-1222.
   - Tip: **Have the container ready:** Tell scouts that Poison Help will ask what the product is, so keep it nearby when you call.
2. **Where Does It Live?** · den · 8 min · Reqs: 2 (set-up)
   - Say: "Every product has a safe home. Sort your cards to the right sign."
   - How: 1) Pairs **run each card** to a storage sign (Option A). With real products (Option B), pairs **point to the sign**, and an adult carries the product there. 2) The den **checks** each choice. 3) Bring up the rules: **original container**, away from food, **up high or locked**, and medicine counts too.
   - Tip: **Home hunt:** Hand out Hazard Labels stickers for scouts to use at home with a parent.
   - (at home) Req 2: find, check and read labels with a parent.
3. **Safe Space Patrol** · den · 17 min · Reqs: 3
   - Say: "Tonight we're safety inspectors for our own meeting place. Find the good stuff and anything that needs fixing."
   - How: 1) Buddy pairs get a **clipboard and checklist**. 2) The **whole den walks together** with both adults, checking exits and exit signs, fire extinguishers, the first-aid kit, lighting, trip hazards and a phone for emergencies. 3) **Regroup** and list what's working and what needs fixing. 4) Each scout names **two ways** we keep this space safe.
   - Tip: **Follow up:** Leaders pass any concerns to the pack committee and the building contact, as the official activity says.
4. **Clean-Up & Handoff** · closing · 5 min · Reqs: none
   - Say: "Inspectors, leave our room safe, with no trip hazards!"
   - How: 1) **Two-minute clean-up**. With Option B, an adult **counts the products back into the bin**, closes it and takes it out of the room before siblings arrive for pickup. 2) Hand each parent the **home-hunt note**. 3) **Hand off** each scout by name.
   - Tip: **Parent in hand:** Give the note to the adult, not the scout.
   - (at home) Req 1: the video with a parent.
