# Fishing electives, all ranks (DRAFT 2, 40-minute den time)

Researched 2026-09-30 by the cubmaster-program agent, using the format of Cycling (draft 2) and Let's Camp! (draft 2). This is the second theme in the elective pass: one adventure that every rank has under its own name. Every plan here is written in our own words. Requirements are only summarized, so use the official page for the full text. Anything marked "(check)" still needs confirming. Nothing is in the app yet.

How these plans fit a Pack 569 den night:
- The pack does the opening ceremony together: flag, Pledge, Oath and Law. Each den then splits off for about 40 minutes.
- Den meetings here have no gathering or opening steps. Each one is den steps plus one short den closing. The minutes add up to 35–40 and never go over 40.
- "Reqs: N" means the step does real work on that requirement. "Reqs: N (set-up)" means the step only sets up or reinforces it and does not complete it.
- The guides don't tick requirements. Leaders record progress on the app's Advancement board afterward.
- **Three kinds of meeting:**
  - **Indoor den nights** (40 min) in the usual den room: safety rules, fish ID, bait, drawing, regulations, planning, knots.
  - **Casting nights** (40 min, Wolf and Bear only). Scouts bring rods from home with the hook taken off. The den casts at targets on grass or in a gym. Each casting night has a "Where:" line. The place is the den leader's choice, **coordinated with the Cubmaster** (Keith, 2026-09-30).
  - **Den fishing outings** (longer than 40 minutes, usually Saturday mornings) for the "go fishing" requirement. Every outing follows the council outing process (check).
- **Six ranks, one theme.** The official names are Go Fish (Lion), Fish On (Tiger), A Wolf Goes Fishing (Wolf), A Bear Goes Fishing (Bear), Catch the Big One (Webelos) and Fishing (AoL). All six match the app exactly, including the lowercase "the" in Catch the Big One. They also match the official fishing blog post (Aug 2026) and the Michigan Crossroads requirement sheets (Oct 2024). No rename is needed.
- **Where to fish:**
  - The official safety activities say to fish **only on still water: ponds or lakes**. Never fish where people are swimming, and get the owner's or manager's permission first (every official page).
  - The den leader picks **a local pond or lake (check)**, coordinated with the Cubmaster. Look for a gentle bank or a railed dock, restrooms, shade, parking, and room to space scouts 10 feet apart (the official Tiger Dry Run Fishing activity allows 10 feet of shore per scout).
  - **The pack's campouts:**
    - **Fort Yargo State Park** (the pack campout, late March) is on 260-acre Lake Marbury. The park allows fishing anywhere along the lake **except the beach**, from shore trails, the docks by the boat ramps and the Visitor Center, and the Area B piers. The campground dock is for campers only. Visitors bring their own tackle. The park lists largemouth bass, catfish, bream, crappie and shad (gastateparks.org).
    - **Camp Rainey Mountain** (council spring family weekend, late April): fishing on the Lake Toccoa shoreline is one of the camp's program stations (the council's camp page and the app's trip page).
    - **Scoutland on Lake Lanier** (district fall family weekend, early October): the app's trip page lists fishing as a program station. A search summary of the council's Scoutland page said a license is needed at "17 and older." Georgia law says 16, and the page didn't load for me (check with the council).
    - A council station may be short or crowded. A den leader who uses one for Bear, Webelos or AoL times the actual fishing (check whether the council signs it off).
- **Georgia fishing licenses** (Georgia DNR Wildlife Resources, checked 2026-09-30):
  - **Scouts don't need a license.** Georgia residents under 16 don't need one. A $10 resident youth license is optional. Nonresident youth 15 and under don't need one either.
  - **Adults 16 and over who fish need one:** residents aged 16–64 and every nonresident 16 or over. On the DNR site, a resident one-day license was $5, plus $1 for each extra day (check current prices). Residents 65 and over should check the senior license rules.
  - **Adults who help.** Georgia's pages don't say whether an adult who only baits or casts for a child needs a license. The official Fishing Basics Instructor's Guide (Feb 2026) says anyone 16 or over who fishes *or helps the Scouts fish* on public water must have one.
  - **Leader's choice for adult licenses (Keith, 2026-09-30):** Option A · every adult who baits, casts, unhooks or holds a rod carries a Georgia license. Adults who only do headcount and first aid don't need one. Den chiefs 16 or over who do any of those things need one too. / Option B · schedule the outing on a Georgia free fishing day, so no Georgia-resident adult needs one (a nonresident adult who fishes still needs one; check). The den leader tells families which option the den is using.
  - **Free fishing days:** Georgia residents need no fishing or trout license on public waters on these days. In 2026 they were **June 6, June 13 and September 26**. The 2027 dates aren't posted, but they usually fall in National Fishing and Boating Week in June and on National Hunting and Fishing Day in late September (check). An outing on a free fishing day (Option B) saves every resident adult the fee.
  - **Private ponds:** Georgia doesn't require a license to fish a private pond (eRegulations). Get the owner's permission in writing (check).
  - **Trout license:** only for designated trout waters, which this plan doesn't use.
  - **Limits:** Georgia's size and daily limits apply to anyone who keeps a fish (Fort Yargo's page says so too). Pack 569 releases every fish (see Catch-and-release), so limits are general knowledge the Bears learn for Req 2, not a rule the den has to apply.
- **Water safety:**
  - **Safe Swim Defense** covers swimming and any activity where people go into water **over knee deep** (Guide to Safe Scouting, Aquatics). Shore and dock fishing doesn't need it as long as nobody wades. **Pack 569: nobody wades.** A snagged line gets cut, not fetched.
  - **Safety Afloat** covers boats. This plan has no fishing from boats.
  - **Life jackets:** the official Webelos and AoL safety activities say that non-swimmers fishing from a dock or wall wear a life jacket. **Pack 569 follows that official rule only (Keith, 2026-09-30):** a non-swimmer who fishes from a dock, pier or wall wears a U.S. Coast Guard–approved life jacket sized for them. Families of non-swimmers bring one and tell the den leader. The pack keeps no loaners. Any family may bring one for their scout, but it isn't required beyond the official rule.
  - **Buddies:** every official page uses the buddy system, and buddies must be able to see each other.
  - Leaders walk the site beforehand for steep banks, slippery rocks and drop-offs (official Safety First).
- **Hooks and casting:**
  - **Barbless hooks**, or barbs pinched flat with pliers, and **no treble hooks** (the official Cub Scout Fishing Adventures Supplement, the Fishing Basics guide, and the Lion, Wolf and Bear outing activities). Adults pinch barbs before the outing.
  - **Adults bait the hooks and take fish off them** (official Safety First and Hooked on Safety). Pack 569 applies this at every rank. Webelos and AoL scouts tie their own hook on at the outing, with an adult beside them.
  - **No loose hooks at den meetings.** Every official practice activity takes the hooks off and ties on a flat washer or a practice plug (Cast & Learn, Cast-A-Thon Relay Challenge, Rod and Reel, Dry Run Fishing, Knot Stations). The Webelos and AoL gear meetings follow official supply lists that include barbless hooks.
  - **Leader's choice for hooks at den meetings (Keith, 2026-09-30):** Option A · Sealed packets: barbless hooks stay sealed in their packets. Scouts learn from the packet and never handle a loose hook, and the leader takes the den's packets home. / Option B · No hooks: pictures or empty packets only, with no hooks at the den. This applies to the Webelos and AoL gear meetings and to any other meeting that would bring sealed hooks, such as the Wolf bait tray.
  - **Eye protection and hats:** the supplement calls them highly recommended. Pack 569 requires sunglasses or safety glasses for everyone in the casting area, at casting nights and outings. Families supply them.
  - **Casting manners** (official Safety First and the Fishing Basics guide): look behind you before every cast, keep 10 feet from the next angler, hold the pole upright when not fishing, and call "Fish on!" when reeling one in so others clear the line.
- **Catch-and-release:**
  - **Pack 569: every Scouting fishing activity is catch-and-release** (Keith, 2026-09-30). No fish are kept, and there are no fish fries.
  - The official Lion and Wolf outing activities practice catch-and-release, and the Fishing Basics guide promotes it.
  - Wet your hands before touching a fish, support it under the belly, and get it back in the water fast (Fishing Basics). For sunfish, smooth the spiny fin down from the head. For catfish, keep clear of the side and back spines (official supplement).
  - If a fish swallows the hook, an adult cuts the line close and releases the fish. Don't dig for the hook.
- **Knives:** nothing in this adventure needs one. Scissors or nail clippers cut line (the official tackle-box list has scissors).
  - Keith's rule: a scout who has earned their rank's knife adventure this year (Bear Whittling, AoL Knife Safety) may use a knife under supervision at home, campouts and outings, but never at den meetings, where adults do the cutting. So at the fishing outing, an earned Bear or AoL scout may cut line with their own pocketknife under supervision. The official AoL Hook, Line and Sinker activity agrees for AoL.
  - Lions, Tigers, Wolves and Webelos have no pocketknife adventure in the app's lists, so their knives stay home.
  - No fish are kept, so there's no fish cleaning.
- **Gear comes from home** (Keith: the pack keeps no loaners except tents). The easiest rigs are a short push-button spincast rod or a cane pole, which the supplement suggests for younger scouts.
  - Each rank has a "No rod, or missed the outing" path. A den family may lend a rod if the owner agrees (check). Bears can make a bamboo pole at their casting night.
  - Fort Yargo says visitors bring their own equipment.
- **Timing, weather and first aid:**
  - **When:** fish bite best in April–May and September–October, and a June free fishing day is a strong choice. From June to August, fish early in the morning. Casting nights need daylight, so check the sunset against the den night's end time.
  - **Thunder:** everyone leaves the water at the first rumble. Rods are tall and graphite conducts, so lay them down and get into cars (every official page says no fishing in thunderstorms).
  - **Heat and sun:** families put sunscreen on at home. Bring water and use shade breaks. Heat exhaustion looks like pale skin, heavy sweating and dizziness: move the scout to shade and give water. Heatstroke (hot, flushed skin and confusion) means calling 911.
  - **Ticks:** they're active in Georgia by late March. Adults apply repellent, and everyone gets a tick check after the outing.
  - **Hook injuries (check with a first-aid-trained adult):**
    - A barbless hook caught in the surface of the skin: an adult wearing gloves backs it out the way it went in, washes the spot and bandages it.
    - A hook that's deep, barbed, or in the face, a joint or near the eye: don't pull it. Pad around it, tape it still, and get medical care. Anything in or near an eye: call 911 and don't press on it.
    - Tell the parent about any puncture, and check the tetanus date on the health form (Part B).
  - **Line:** pick up every scrap. Loose line tangles birds.
- **Paperwork:** the official outing activities ask for an Annual Health and Medical Record (Parts A and B) for every participant, youth and adult. Pack 569 also uses an Activity Consent Form for every outing and every off-site casting night, as it does for cycling (check).
- Two registered adults 21 or over in every den room and at every outing, and no one-on-one contact. For Lions and Tigers, an adult partner doesn't count as one of the two unless they are registered as a leader. If any girl attends, one of the two registered adults is a woman 21 or over. At the water, the official outing activities ask for extra adults. Aim for one adult for every two or three Wolves and older (pack suggestion; check).
- **Food:** none is needed. The Wolf meat-stick bait option uses food as bait only, not to eat, with an allergy check first. Webelos and AoL trail food in the essentials stays sealed. No den picnics.
- **Devices:** the official Tiger Our Fishing Spot activity and the Webelos Plan Your Fishing Adventure use a smart device. Pack 569 uses printed aerial maps and printed spot sheets, and the leader's own device checks anything else.
- **Counting twice (Keith):** a den fishing outing between May 1 and August 31 also counts as one of the three Summertime Fun activities for the rank the scout is moving into (see summertime-fun.md). Fishing is one of the official Summertime Fun pack ideas.
- **Pack option:** the official Fishing pack-meeting page is held **at a place where families can fish**. It runs four stations: equipment and casting, fish drawing, hook and bait, and go fishing. Its requirement map has errors: it labels the Tiger safety rules as Req 1 (they're Req 2), gives Webelos and AoL Req 4 the Bear wording, and lists "attach the hook" for Lions, Tigers and Wolves, who have no such requirement. Each rank's "Done at a pack meeting:" line below uses the corrected map (check). A July pack fishing day could serve as every den's outing (see Open questions).
- **Guest speaker:** official options include a DNR representative (Tiger Req 1), a guest speaker on fish or regulations (Bear Reqs 1 and 2), and a guest speaker on local fish (AoL Req 3). One pack guest-speaker night with Georgia DNR Wildlife Resources fisheries staff or a park ranger could serve several dens (check who's available).
- **Leader reading:** every official page points freshwater leaders to the Fishing Basics PowerPoint and Instructor's Guide (v2.1, Feb 2026). Read the guide once before your first outing.
- **Sources.** On 2026-09-30 I read, using the r.jina.ai reader:
  - the six official adventure pages and the official Fishing pack-meeting page
  - the activity pages listed under each rank. The reader showed activity links only on the Lion page, so I found the rest by search and confirmed each one's rank and requirement on the page itself. Some official activities have no page I could find, and they're marked (check). The Tiger outing activity page returned only a maintenance notice.
  - the Cub Scout Fishing Adventures Supplement (Sept 2024) and the Fishing Basics Instructor's Guide (Feb 2026)
  - Guide to Safe Scouting, Aquatics Safety (gss02)
  - Age Appropriate Guidelines (680-685, 08/24): the reader showed fishing checked in every column, and the app's trip rules say fishing is fine for every rank (check the column reading)
  - Georgia DNR "What License Do I Need?", Georgia eRegulations license requirements, the 2026 free fishing day announcements, the Fort Yargo fishing page, and the council's Camp Rainey Mountain page
  - **Cross-checks:**
    - The Michigan Crossroads requirement sheets (Oct 2024) match all six adventures. The one difference is wording: the sheets say "BSA SAFE Checklist" where the current pages say "Scouting America SAFE Checklist."
    - The Webelos Req 1 note that the whole adventure follows the scout's plan appears on the Michigan sheet and on the AoL page. The reader didn't show it on the Webelos page (check).

  2. Draw a fish you think lives where you'll fish. (meeting)
  3. Go fishing with your adult partner. (den outing with the partner there, or with family)
- Done at the pack opening: nothing.

- Leader's choice for Req 1: Option A · Lions Fish Safely: pairs color the six rules on the handbook page, then each Lion says one (official). / Option B · Safe or Not Safe Fishing Rules: the same coloring, then the official yes-or-no quiz read aloud (official). It's the same step with the same minutes.
- Leader's choice for the outing: Option A · a local pond or lake the den leader picks (check), on a Saturday morning. / Option B · a den fishing hour at the Fort Yargo pack campout, from the shore or a dock, never the beach (check the date, and expect cool water and slow bites in late March). Both are coordinated with the Cubmaster.
- Leader's choice for adult licenses: Option A · every adult who baits, casts, unhooks or holds a rod carries a Georgia license; adults who only do headcount and first aid don't need one. / Option B · the outing is on a Georgia free fishing day, so no resident adult needs one (see the header).
- No rod, or missed the outing:
  - A family fishing trip with the adult partner counts, because the official Req 3 names only the partner. Fish still water, follow the six rules, and tell the den leader the date.
  - A den family may lend a rod or cane pole, if the owner agrees (check).
  - The pack fishing day, if the pack holds one.
- Safety notes:
  - The partner stays at their Lion's elbow at both meetings. No drop-offs.
  - No hooks at Meeting 1. The rod on show has a practice plug.
  - At the water: barbless hooks, adults bait and unhook, pairs 10 feet apart, and life jackets for non-swimmers on docks, piers and walls.
  - Sunglasses or safety glasses and a hat for every Lion and partner.
  - Health forms (Parts A and B) for everyone, and a consent form for the outing.

  - Fill in the health form (Parts A and B) for your Lion and yourself, and sign the consent form.

### Meeting 1 of 2 · Safe Fishing and My Fish · 40 min
- All-dens option: the Lion and Tiger dens can share this night. Lion Reqs 1 and 2 match Tiger Reqs 2 and 3. Each den's leaders run their own pairs.
- Prep: 2–3 weeks before the outing:
  - Pick the pond or lake first, coordinated with the Cubmaster. Find out which fish live there from the manager, the DNR or a local tackle shop (the official activity says to research this first; check).
  - Print a big photo of the spot and pictures of two or three of its fish (bluegill, largemouth bass and catfish are common in Georgia ponds; check).
  - Get Lion handbooks, or print the official activity pages. The activities cite handbook pages 38, 39 and 41 (check the pages in your edition). Print the yes-or-no quiz for Option B.
  - Bring one rod with a practice plug and no hook, a bobber, and a child's life jacket to show.
  - Print the family fishing note.
- Supplies: spot photo, fish pictures, handbooks or printouts, quiz (Option B), crayons, pencils, craft scissors, a rod with a plug, bobber, life jacket, family fishing notes
- Tell parents before they leave: The outing is [date/place] (check). Please read the note: rods without hooks, sunglasses, a life jacket if your Lion can't swim, and whether adults who fish need a license (the den leader will tell you).
1. **Where We're Fishing** · den · 5 min · Reqs: 2 (set-up)
   - Say: "This is the pond where we're going fishing! Who do you think lives in there?"
   - How: 1) Show the **spot photo**. 2) Hold up the **fish pictures** and name each one. 3) Lions **point** to the one they want to catch.
   - Tip: **Keep the photo up** for the drawing step.
2. **Six Safe Fishing Rules** · den · 12 min · Reqs: 1
   - Say: "Fishers have six rules that keep everyone safe. Let's find them in your book."
   - How: **Option A · Lions Fish Safely:** 1) Pairs **color** each rule panel. 2) Say each **rule** as they color: a grown-up with you, permission to fish there, check the weather, keep your buddy in sight, give others room, and never fish where people swim. 3) Each Lion **says one rule** until all six are covered. / **Option B · Safe or Not Safe:** 1) Pairs **color** the panels. 2) Read the **yes-or-no quiz**. 3) Lions **raise a hand** for "safe."
   - Tip: **Note Req 1** for each Lion who can tell their partner a rule.
3. **Draw My Fish** · den · 13 min · Reqs: 2
   - Say: "Which fish lives in OUR pond? Draw it with your grown-up."
   - How: 1) Lions **color and cut out** the handbook fish (official Drawing Fish With A Grid). 2) Pairs **draw** a fish square by square using the grid. 3) Each Lion **names** their fish.
   - Tip: **Note Req 2** for each Lion who drew a fish from our water. **Scissors:** craft scissors only, with the partner helping.
4. **Outing Preview** · den · 5 min · Reqs: 3 (set-up)
   - Say: "At the pond, your grown-up puts the bait on, you watch the bobber, and every fish goes back home."
   - How: 1) Show the **rod and plug**, and the **bobber**. 2) Show a **life jacket** buckled on a partner, and explain that non-swimmers wear one on docks, piers and walls. 3) Practice a **pretend release**: wet hands, gentle, back in the water.
   - Tip: **Hooks stay home tonight.** Say that adults do the hooks.
5. **Clean-Up & Check-Out** · closing · 5 min · Reqs: none
   - Say: "Lions, pick up three scraps with your grown-up."
   - How: 1) **Clean up**. 2) Put a **family fishing note** in each partner's hand. 3) **Check out** each Lion with their partner by name.
   - Tip: **Drawings** go home to bring to the outing.
   - (at home) Non-swimmers try on their life jacket. Everyone practices the six rules.

### Meeting 2 of 2 · Lion Fishing Outing (outing, about 90 min)
- Where: the pond or lake the den leader picked (Option A or B above; check), on a Saturday morning.
- Prep: 2–3 weeks before:
  - Follow the council outing process (check). Get permission from the owner or manager. Collect health forms (Parts A and B) and consent forms.
  - Visit the site a week before: a gentle bank or railed dock, restrooms, shade, parking, and no swimmers nearby (official Lion Go Fishing).
  - Check the forecast. Move the date for thunderstorms or high heat.
  - Confirm two registered adults 21 or over. Adult licenses: under Option A, confirm that every adult who will bait, cast, unhook or hold a rod has one; under Option B, the date is a free fishing day.
  - Know which Lions are non-swimmers.
  - Pinch the barbs on every hook, and bring worms or bait from a bait shop.
- Supplies: roster (non-swimmers marked), health and consent forms, first-aid kit (non-latex gloves, wipes, bandages, gauze, tape), barbless hooks, bobbers, split shot, bait, pliers, line clippers, a hook container, whistle, water jug, trash bag
- Tell parents before they leave: Great fishing! If today was between May 1 and August 31, it also counts as one of the three for Tiger Summertime Fun.
1. **Arrival & Safety Check** · den · 10 min · Reqs: 1 (set-up)
   - Say: "Welcome, fishers! Hats, sunglasses, and life jackets for non-swimmers going on the dock."
   - How: 1) **Count** Lions and partners. 2) Check **hats and eye protection**, and **life jackets on non-swimmers**. 3) Walk the **fishing zone** and point out the no-go spots.
   - Tip: **Non-swimmer with no life jacket?** That Lion fishes from the flat bank, away from docks, piers and walls.
2. **Rules at the Water** · den · 5 min · Reqs: 1 (set-up)
   - Say: "Which of our six rules do you see right here?"
   - How: 1) Pairs **name** the rules they can spot. 2) Space pairs **10 feet apart**. 3) Practice **"look behind you"** before a cast.
   - Tip: **Poles up** when you're walking.
3. **Go Fish** · den · 55 min · Reqs: 3
   - Say: "Watch your bobber. When it dips, lift the pole and reel slowly!"
   - How: 1) Adults **bait** the barbless hooks. 2) Partners **cast** or help cast. 3) Lions **watch and reel**. 4) Adults **unhook**, and every fish goes **back** with wet hands. 5) Take a **shade and water break** halfway.
   - Tip: **Hook in skin?** A barbless hook in the surface backs out the way it went in. If it's deep or near an eye, don't pull it (see the header).
4. **Our Fish, Our Drawings** · den · 5 min · Reqs: 2 (set-up)
   - Say: "Did any fish look like your drawing?"
   - How: 1) Lions **compare** the fish they saw to their drawings. 2) **Name** each fish caught.
   - Tip: **No bites?** Talk about where fish hide: weeds, logs and shade.
5. **Pack-Up & Line Sweep** · den · 5 min · Reqs: none
   - Say: "Fishers leave no line behind. Let's find every scrap."
   - How: 1) Adults put every **hook** in the container. 2) Pairs **sweep** for line and trash.
   - Tip: **Line scraps** go in the trash bag, never on the ground.
6. **Headcount & Check-Out** · closing · 5 min · Reqs: 3
   - Say: "You went fishing like real Scouts!"
   - How: 1) **Count** everyone. 2) **Check out** each Lion with their partner by name.
   - Tip: **Note Req 3** for each Lion who fished with their partner. Take attendance in the app.
   - (at home) Tick check tonight.

## Fish On (Tiger)

### Meeting 3 of 3 · The Patrol Fishing Outing (outing, about 110 min)
- Where: the spot the patrol chose at Meeting 1, on a Saturday morning (check).
- Prep: 2–3 weeks before:
  - Follow the council outing process (check). Get permission, and collect health and consent forms.
  - Walk the site. Arrange extra adults. Adult licenses: confirm them (Option A) or pick a free fishing day (Option B). Know which scouts are non-swimmers.
  - Start by 9 a.m. in warm months.
- Supplies: roster (non-swimmers marked), buddy list, forms, SAFE chart, first-aid kit, extra barbless hooks, bait, pliers, line clippers, a hook container, a timer (the leader's watch or phone), whistle, water jug, trash bag
- Tell parents before they leave: If today fell between May 1 and August 31, it may also count toward Summertime Fun for any scout still earning it (check).
1. **Arrival & SAFE Check** · den · 10 min · Reqs: 2 (set-up)
   - Say: "Denner, read the SAFE chart. Is everything here?"
   - How: 1) **Count** everyone. 2) The denner **reads** the chart and the patrol checks each item, including **life jackets on non-swimmers**. 3) Pair **buddies**.
   - Tip: **Matches stay home.** Adults carry the first-aid kit.
2. **Rig Up** · den · 10 min · Reqs: 5 (set-up)
   - Say: "Tie on with your knot. Hook by the eye."
   - How: 1) Scouts **tie** on their barbless hooks. 2) Adults **test** the knots and **bait**.
   - Tip: **Knife Safety earned this year?** Cut your tag end with your own knife, with an adult watching. Otherwise use clippers.
3. **Fish for an Hour** · den · 70 min · Reqs: 7
   - Say: "An hour at least, where your habitat plan says the fish are. Follow Georgia's rules."
   - How: 1) Scouts **choose** spots 10 feet apart. 2) A leader **times** the fishing and stops the clock for breaks. 3) Adults **unhook**, and scouts release fish with **wet hands**. 4) **Water break** at 30 minutes.
   - Tip: **Keep fishing** until the clock passes 60 minutes.
4. **Start, Stop, Continue** · den · 5 min · Reqs: 1 (set-up)
   - Say: "What should the next patrol start, stop and keep doing?"
   - How: 1) Each scout gives **one** start, stop or continue. 2) The denner **writes** them down.
   - Tip: **Save the list** in the den box.
5. **Line Sweep** · den · 10 min · Reqs: none
   - Say: "Leave no trace, and no line."
   - How: 1) Adults **box the hooks**. 2) Buddies **sweep**.
   - Tip: **Count the hooks** back in.
6. **Headcount & Handoff** · closing · 5 min · Reqs: 7
   - Say: "That's Fishing, patrol!"
   - How: 1) **Count** everyone. 2) **Hand off** each scout to their adult by name.
   - Tip: **Note Req 7** for each scout who fished an hour or more. Take attendance in the app.
