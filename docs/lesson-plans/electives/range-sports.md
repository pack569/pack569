# Archery, BB Gun and Slingshot: electives, all ranks (DRAFT 1)

> **A guide, not the rulebook.** These are ideas to help a den leader run the meeting. They are not a complete or official source. The official requirements on scouting.org and the Guide to Safe Scouting always come first. If something here is unclear or unsettled, use your judgment and check with the Cubmaster.

Researched 2026-09-30 by the cubmaster-program agent, using the format of Summertime Fun (draft 1) and Let's Camp! (draft 2). This is the next elective in the pass. Every plan here is in our own words. Requirements are only summarized, so use the official lesson plans for the full text. Anything marked "(check)" still needs confirming. Nothing is in the app yet.

How these adventures fit a Pack 569 den night:
- **They don't.** Every rank has **zero den meetings** for all three range sports. Keith's ruling (2026-09-30): no part of Archery, BB Guns or Slingshot is done at a den meeting, and that includes the non-shooting parts (range rules, commands, gear, parts of the equipment). The official material says the same thing:
  - Each official rank page lists these three adventures on their own, under "Special Elective Adventures," and says they can only be completed at approved events with qualified instructors. That covers the whole adventure, not just the shooting.
  - Scouting America's Cub Scout range page says Cub Scout range and target activities happen at district and council events only. It adds that archery, BB guns and slingshots are "not approved unit activities."
  - The 2026 Range and Target Activities Manual (p. 20) says the same, and says these activities are prohibited at the pack level.
  - The official requirements appear only in the three rangemaster lesson plans (May 2024). Each plan is written for a rangemaster at a council day camp, resident camp, short-term camp or one-day event, and each says units may not hold range activities at the den or pack level. The rangemaster teaches the gear, rules, commands and equipment parts in Session 1 at the range. The plans give dens no pre-camp part.
- **No equipment, ever.** No bows, arrows, BB guns, slingshots, BBs or ammunition come to a den meeting or a pack meeting, not even as props. Toy versions don't come either. No official source allows an inert demo, so the answer is no.
- **Not at home either.** A family shooting in the backyard is not a Cub Scout activity and doesn't count toward any requirement. The adventure is done only on a council- or district-run range.
- **Where it happens:** council day and twilight camps, council resident and short-term camps, and council or district events with a trained rangemaster. The rangemaster must be 18 or over and trained through the National Camping School or the NRA (the Cub Scout range page, and the Range and Target Activities Manual p. 9). Each rank block has a "Done at a council range:" line, and the den leader confirms who took part from the camp's list.
- **Pack campouts:**
  - Scoutland (the Apalachee District fall weekend) and Camp Rainey Mountain (the council spring weekend) are district and council events. Their ranges are run by district or council staff, so shooting there counts.
  - **Fort Yargo is the pack's own campout, so it has no range and never can.** The app's Fort Yargo page already says so (`CAMP_PACK_RUN`).
- **Which ranks:**
  - **Archery:** all six ranks. Officially it's "Archery Lion," "Archery Tiger" and so on up to "Archery Arrow of Light."
  - **Slingshot:** all six ranks, with one name, "Slingshot."
  - **BB Guns:** Tiger through AoL. **Lions have no BB Guns.** The official Lion page lists only Archery Lion and Slingshot. The BB lesson plan says the adventure is for every rank except Lions, and the manual's table says "All program levels except Lion."
  - The app's `ADVENTURES` (~line 7585) matches this. The one difference is the name: the app says "BB Gun" and the official pages say "BB Guns." See Open questions.
- **Lions and Tigers:** the adult partner goes with them to the range. The archery and slingshot plans say Lions and Tigers must have their partner with them there. The BB plan says the same for Tigers. At the range, the partner helps with Reqs 1–5.
- **Partial work carries over:**
  - The archery plan says arrows can be shot across several sessions.
  - The slingshot plan says each scout should get a list of what they finished, to bring to the next council event.
  - The BB plan says the whole adventure can take two sessions or fewer.
  - All three plans are built around five 45-minute day-camp sessions.
- **Which rank's version, and when (check).**
  - The Scouting America systems move every scout up a grade on June 1 (the 2024 program FAQ, as noted in summertime-fun.md). So June and July camps most likely count for the rank the scout is *moving into*. A rising Wolf at June day camp earns Wolf Archery.
  - That means this year's dens get two chances: **Scoutland, Oct 2–4, 2026 (this weekend)** and **Rainey Mountain Spring Family Camping (late April 2027; 2026 was Apr 24–26)**.
  - **Look back at summer 2026:** a scout who shot at a June 2026 day camp or at Cub Summer Splash in July 2026 may already have finished their *current* rank's version. Ask families for the camp's completion list.
- **Lions can only use the family weekends.** NEGA's day and twilight camps take scouts entering grades 1–5, and Cub Summer Splash takes rising Tigers through AoL. By June a Lion is already a rising Tiger. Whether the Scoutland and Rainey ranges let Lions shoot needs checking.
- **NEGA events and their ranges (2026 pages; 2027 dates aren't posted, check):**
  - **Keith (2026-09-30): at Scoutland, Lions shoot bows and arrows, and Tigers and up shoot BB guns.** Slingshot is still unconfirmed.
  - **Apalachee Fall Family Camping, Scoutland, Oct 2–4, 2026.** The page lists "Shooting Sports" but doesn't say which kinds. The app's Scoutland page says BB guns and archery (check). It's a whole-family event. The fee was $30 early and $35 by Sept 30, and it's now $45 on site.
  - **Spring Family Camping, Camp Rainey Mountain, Apr 24–26, 2026.** The page lists "BB's and Archery" and "Partial Adventure Achievements." It's a whole-family event. Adults 21 and over could take BB and Archery Rangemaster Training there on Saturday.
  - **Cub Scout day and twilight camps (June).** NEGA's list says activities may include bows and arrows and BB guns. It doesn't say which camps have which range (check with the Buford Day Camp and Lawrenceville Twilight Camp directors). They're for scouts entering grades 1–5, and a Tiger must bring a parent or adult.
  - **Cub Summer Splash, Camp Rainey Mountain, July 17–19, 2026.** Scouts "shoot bow and arrow or BB guns." It's for rising Tigers through AoL and cost $175 per scout and $50 per adult.
  - **Scoutland Summer Quest "Range & Target," June 27, 2026, 1:30–4:30 p.m.** It was new in 2026. The page doesn't say which ranges it covers (check). Parents must attend. It cost $30 per scout.
  - **Slingshot:** no NEGA page I could read names a slingshot range (check). If no NEGA event runs one, no Pack 569 scout can earn Slingshot this year. See Open questions.
- **What to wear and bring (for the family notes):**
  - **Eye protection:** required for everyone on the BB and slingshot ranges, participants and staff alike (the BB and slingshot lesson plans, and the manual).
  - **Archery gear:** an arm guard on the bow arm, plus a finger tab or glove (the manual). The manual's archery table doesn't list eye protection (check each camp's rule).
  - **Who supplies what:** ranges usually supply the equipment and protective gear (check with each camp).
  - **Closed-toe shoes and hair tied back:** usual camp range rules, but I didn't find them in the national sources (check each camp).
- **Staffing ratios** (manual's table, check): BB range one adult per Tiger; slingshot range one adult per Cub Scout at every rank; archery one per eight. Council ranges may ask parents to help on the line.
- **Don't mix in older materials:**

  - The official range safety moment has a line saying Tigers, Wolves and Bears "can only shoot BB guns." It conflicts with the 2026 manual and the lesson plans, so these plans follow the manual.
  - The Guide to Safe Scouting's range section (gss08) now just points to the Range and Target Activities Manual. That manual is the 2026 renaming of the National Shooting Sports Manual.
- "Reqs: N" isn't used here because there are no den steps. The guides don't tick requirements. Leaders record completions on the app's Advancement board and in Scoutbook Plus from the camp's list or the rangemaster's sign-off.
- Two registered adults 21 or over at any den gathering, including a den that goes to a council event together. No one-on-one contact. For Lions and Tigers, an adult partner doesn't count as one of the two unless they are registered as a leader. If any girl attends, one of the two registered adults is a woman 21 or over.

## Archery (Lion)
- Elective · Meetings: 0 (council range only) · Official page: https://www.scouting.org/programs/cub-scouts/adventures/lion/ ("Archery Lion," under Special Elective Adventures; no adventure page) · Requirements: https://www.scouting.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/2024_Cub-Scout_Archery-Adventure-Lesson-Plan_051624.pdf · Other sources: https://www.scouting.org/outdoor-programs/range-activities/cub-scout-shooting-sports/, https://www.scouting.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/430-93825b-Range-and-Target-Activities-Manual-FINAL.pdf, https://www.scouting.org/outdoor-programs/range-activities/range-activities-faq/, https://www.nega-bsa.org/APFF, https://www.nega-bsa.org/family-camp · Checked: 2026-09-30
- Summary: The Lion and their adult partner learn range safety and shoot a few arrows on a council archery range. The rangemaster teaches everything. A Lion's only realistic chances are the two family weekends: Scoutland this weekend and Rainey Mountain in April. By summer camp season the Lion is a rising Tiger.
- Requirements (own words):
  1. Name and put on the right safety gear. (council range; with the partner)
  2. Show you know the range rules and whistle signals. (council range; with the partner)
  3. Show the range commands. (council range; with the partner)
  4. Name the bow and arrow's main parts and their uses. (council range; with the partner)
  5. Show a safe stance and good shooting form. (council range; with the partner)
  6. Shoot rounds of 2 arrows, at least 4, trying to improve. (council range)
  7. Show how to fetch your arrows safely. (council range)
  8. Talk about putting the gear away and storing it. (council range)
- Done at the pack opening: nothing.
- Done at a pack meeting: nothing. Once it's recorded, the adventure can be presented at a pack meeting.
- Done at a council range: Reqs 1–8, all of them. Likely events: Apalachee Fall Family Camping at Scoutland (Oct 2–4, 2026: Lions shoot archery there, per Keith) and Spring Family Camping at Camp Rainey Mountain (late April 2027, check; "BB's and Archery" in 2026). The partner stays with the Lion on the range. The den leader confirms who took part from the camp's list. Fort Yargo has no range.
- Leader's choice for how the den takes part: Option A · Den goes together: the den picks one family weekend, and the den leader and a second registered adult camp near each other and walk the Lions to the range as a group. / Option B · Families go on their own: each family signs up, and the den leader collects the camp's lists afterward.
- What the den leader does:
  - Tell families which weekends have archery, with the dates and registration deadlines.
  - Remind them about closed-toe shoes and hair tied back. The range supplies the arm guard and finger tab (check).
  - Remind them that the partner goes to the range with the Lion.
  - Record completions only from the camp's list or the rangemaster's sign-off, on the Advancement board and in Scoutbook Plus. Keep any partial list for the next council event.
  - Never bring archery gear to a den meeting, and don't teach any part of it there.
- Safety notes:
  - Only the rangemaster gives commands. Everyone follows the whistle.
  - The partner is at the Lion's side the whole time.
  - Nobody goes forward of the shooting line until the rangemaster says so.
  - Two registered adults 21 or over with a den that goes together. No one-on-one. If any girl attends, one of the two registered adults is a woman 21 or over.
- Family note (send by email now, and again in March):
  - Archery is a Lion elective, but our den can't do it at den meetings, and neither can the pack. Scouting America allows Cub Scout archery only on a range run by the council or district with a trained rangemaster. That includes the safety lessons. It isn't us being cautious. It's the rule, and it's why we don't practice with toy bows either.
  - Your Lion can earn it at a council family weekend: Scoutland, Oct 2–4 (on-site registration $45 per family; Lions shoot archery there), or Camp Rainey Mountain in late April (date: check).
  - You go to the range with your Lion and help with the safety steps.
  - Wear closed-toe shoes and tie back long hair. The range provides the bows, arrows and arm guards (check).
  - Backyard archery is fun, but it doesn't count toward the adventure.
  - Ask the range for the list of what your Lion finished and send it to the den leader. Anything left over can be finished at the next council event.
