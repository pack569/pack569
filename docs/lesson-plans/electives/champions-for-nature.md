# Champions for Nature: elective, all ranks (DRAFT 2, 40-minute den time)

Researched 2026-09-30 by the cubmaster-program agent, using the format of Race Time, the first elective file. This is the second elective in the pass. Every plan here is written in our own words. Requirements are only summarized, so use the official page for the full text. Anything marked "(check)" still needs confirming. Nothing is in the app yet.

Draft 2 (2026-09-30) adds Keith's answers on four points: the conservation project, where made items go, devices, and AoL food. On 2026-09-30 I re-read the official Food Miles Fruit Salad page (via r.jina.ai) for the AoL changes.

How these plans fit a Pack 569 den night:
- The pack does the opening ceremony together: flag, Pledge, Oath and Law. Each den then splits off for about 40 minutes.
- Den meetings here have no gathering or opening steps. Each one is den steps plus one short den closing. The minutes add up to 35–40 and never go over 40.
- No Champions for Nature requirement is done at the pack opening. Some can be done at a pack meeting: the Wolf Recycling Roundup, the AoL Pumpkin Drive, and the optional guest-speaker nights. Those have a "Done at a pack meeting:" line, and the den leader confirms who took part.
- "Reqs: N" means the step does real work on that requirement. "Reqs: N (set-up)" means the step only sets up or reinforces it and does not complete it.
- The guides don't tick requirements. Leaders record progress on the app's Advancement board afterward.
- **This is not a Leave No Trace adventure.** Each rank has its own topic:
  - Lion: natural versus made by people, kinds of trash, recycling.
  - Tiger: renewable resources, the 3 R's, where garbage goes.
  - Wolf: renewable resources, the 3 R's, where recyclables go.
  - Bear: natural resources, wastewater, soil, air pollution.
  - Webelos: habitats and one threatened or endangered animal.
  - Arrow of Light: local food, food deserts and oases, food waste.
  - Every rank ends with a conservation project.
- **The conservation project: the den leader chooses.** Every rank has two options, and neither one is the default (Keith, 2026-09-30):
  - Option A: the all-dens April Scouting for Clean Waterways cleanup, which is an outing.
  - Option B: a project the den picks for itself from the official list. Each rank's plan gives one that fits:
    - Lion: bee pots.
    - Tiger: bottle planters.
    - Wolf: the Recycling Roundup.
    - Bear: a planting outing. No official Bear project fits a den room, so Bears need an outing under either option.
    - Webelos: bee hotels.
    - AoL: the Pumpkin Drive, or a partner service outing.
  - Under Option A, the den-night project steps still run as practice or set-up, and the conservation requirement is noted at the cleanup.
- **All-dens outing: the April Scouting for Clean Waterways cleanup.** Every rank's official page lists a Clean Waterways activity for its conservation requirement. The national Cub Scout page says:
  - A cleanup counts toward Champions for Nature.
  - It can be held anywhere there's a need, such as parks, school grounds or a house of worship.
  - It should last only 1–2 hours.
  - Logging it in Scoutbook earns a certificate, and a patch is sold at the Scout Shop.
  - Most units run it April–June. Pack 569 holds it in April (Keith, 2026-09-30).
  - The app's standard year already has an April "Spring hike / service project" (`SEED_ACTIVITIES`), which fits. Each rank's section has its own outing plan, so it still stands alone.
  - Official safety rules:
    - Gloves for everyone.
    - Adults pick up anything sharp or heavy.
    - A 5-gallon bucket for sharp items.
    - The buddy system.
    - Health forms reviewed.
    - A first-aid kit.
  - Pack 569 adds: permission from the landowner, the Service Project Planning Checklist, the SAFE Checklist, and the council outing process (check).
- **Plants, planters and bee hotels: the den leader chooses** (Keith, 2026-09-30). Option A: they go home. Option B: they're placed at the chartered organization (the local church), with its permission. This applies to the Lion bee pots, the Tiger bottle planters and the Webelos bee hotels, and each of those ranks has it as a one-line leader's choice.
- **Outings** aren't normal den nights. They keep their own length and are marked "(outing, about N min)".
- **Guest speakers.** No Champions for Nature requirement requires meeting an expert, so the den step is always Option A, and a pack guest-speaker night is Option B where it fits. One guest from the local recycling or solid-waste program (check) could cover Lion, Tiger and Wolf Req 3 on the same night. The guest-speaker dates are unknown.
- **Tools on service projects** (2026 SAFE Project Tool Use chart, 680-028):
  - Lions and Tigers use no tools on service projects. Lions fill pots with gloved hands or cups while adults use the trowels.
  - Rakes, shovels and trowels start at Wolf/Bear, and Webelos and AoL may use them too. Bears use shovels only, and adults move the wheelbarrows.
  - Wheelbarrows, handsaws, electric screwdrivers and palm sanders are Scouts BSA only, so adults use them.
  - No youth works 4 feet or more above the ground.
  - The official Lion and Tiger pages say Lions and Tigers may not use paintbrushes on a service project, although crafts are fine.
- **Planting season.** The official seed-bomb activity says to plant in spring and check your area's growing season. In Georgia, fall and early spring are usually the planting windows for native perennials and trees (check with UGA Extension). Avoid scheduling the Lion bee pots or the Bear planting in deep winter or mid-summer heat.
- **Devices: the leader's device only** (Keith, 2026-09-30). Scouts don't use phones or tablets at den meetings. For the Webelos and AoL research, the leader shows the material on their own device or brings printouts. When the leader shows a website, the official rules still apply: an adult controls all internet use, and parents are told first.
- **Food.** Only AoL deals with food. Keith allows a no-cook food activity with an allergy check (2026-09-30), so AoL Meeting 1 offers the official Food Miles Fruit Salad for Req 2 as a leader's choice. The rules:
  - Nothing is cooked at den meetings. Keith's cooking rule: meals are cooked at home, at a pack campout, or at a Webelos/AoL den or patrol campout, with adults running the stoves.
  - Check allergies before buying the fruit.
  - Everyone washes their hands before touching food.
  - Knives (Keith's rule): a scout who has earned their rank's knife adventure this year (Bear Whittling, AoL Knife Safety) may use a knife under supervision at home, campouts and outings, but never at den meetings, where adults do the cutting. So at the den, adults cut the fruit, or the leader buys it pre-cut.
  - The official Food Preservation activity (apple chips) needs cooking, so it stays out.
- Two registered adults 21 or over in every den room, and no one-on-one contact, including online. For Lions and Tigers, an adult partner doesn't count as one of the two unless they are registered as a leader. If any girl attends, one of the two registered adults is a woman 21 or over.
- **Sources.** On 2026-09-30 I read all six official Champions for Nature pages and the activity pages listed under each rank on scouting.org, using the r.jina.ai reader. I cross-checked all six ranks against the Michigan Crossroads Council's Oct 2024 requirement PDFs. They match, except for two points:
  - **Lion Req 2:** the official page lists organic, paper, plastic, metal and glass waste. The Michigan PDF, and the official Types of Trash activity, name only organic, paper and glass. This plan teaches all five (check).
  - **Tiger Req 4:** the official page says "conservation project," and the Michigan PDF says "conservation service project." It's the same work either way.
  - "Champions for Nature" appears in the electives list of all six ranks in the app's `ADVENTURES`, spelled the same way.

### Meeting 1 of 2 · Nature Made It or People Made It? · 40 min
- Prep: 1–2 weeks before:
  - Fill one pillowcase for every two or three pairs, each with a mix of natural items (stick, rock, feather, leaf, pinecone, flower) and things people made (spoon, water bottle, flashlight, towel, trash bag). This follows the official sorting game.
  - Set two boxes about 15 feet away: one with a tree picture for "nature made it" and one with a house picture for "people made it."
  - For the official Natural to Man-Made talk, bring a wooden pencil, a cotton T-shirt, a wool sock and a glass cup.
  - For Req 2, set out five picture bins: food scraps (a banana peel in a zip bag), paper, plastic, metal and glass. Put 3–4 clean items in each.
- Supplies: pillowcases, 2 picture boxes, natural and made items, pencil, cotton T-shirt, wool sock, glass cup, 5 picture bins, clean trash items, zip bags, hand sanitizer
- Tell parents before they leave: This week, let your Lion help sort your recycling at home. At Meeting 2 on [date] we plant flowers for bees, so wear clothes that can get dirty. Please tell me about any bee-sting allergy.
1. **Who Made It?** · den · 8 min · Reqs: 1
   - Say: "Some things come right from nature. Other things were made by people. Point to the tree if nature made it, and to the house if people made it!"
   - How: 1) Hold up **one item at a time**. 2) Lions **point** to the tree sign or the house sign. 3) Ask "**How do you know?**" 4) Do about **eight items**.
   - Tip: **Easy clue:** Could you find it outside on a walk, just like this? Then nature made it.
2. **Sort Dash** · den · 12 min · Reqs: 1
   - Say: "Your grown-up pulls something out of the bag. You speed-walk it to the right box and come back for the next one!"
   - How: 1) The partner **pulls one item**. 2) The Lion **speed-walks** it to a box. 3) A leader at the boxes **checks it**, and a wrong box means trying again. 4) Keep going until **every bag is empty**, then look in both boxes together.
   - Tip: **Note Req 1** for each Lion who sorts most items correctly with their partner.
3. **Made From Nature** · den · 5 min · Reqs: 1
   - Say: "People make things, but they start with something from nature. Guess where this came from!"
   - How: 1) Show the **pencil**, made from a tree. 2) Show the **T-shirt**, from a cotton plant. 3) Show the **sock**, from a sheep's wool. 4) An adult holds the **glass cup**, made from sand.
   - Tip: **Keep the glass up high:** Only adults hold it.
4. **Trash Sort** · den · 10 min · Reqs: 2
   - Say: "Trash isn't all the same. Let's put each piece in its own bin: food scraps, paper, plastic, metal or glass."
   - How: 1) Name each **bin** and what happens to it: food scraps rot back into soil, paper comes from trees, plastic is made from oil, and metal and glass can be melted and made again. 2) The partner **hands over one item**, and the Lion **drops it** in a bin. 3) Adults place the **glass and metal** items while the Lion points.
   - Tip: **Note Req 2** for each Lion who can name the five kinds with their partner.
5. **Clean-Up & Check-Out** · closing · 5 min · Reqs: none
   - Say: "Lions, let's sort our own clean-up the same way!"
   - How: 1) Lions put tonight's items **back in the right bins**. 2) Everyone uses **hand sanitizer**. 3) **Check out** each Lion with their partner by name.
   - Tip: **Count the pillowcases** so none go home by mistake.
   - (at home) Req 3 (set-up): the Lion helps sort the family's recycling.

### Meeting 2 of 2 · Recycle It and Feed the Bees · 40 min
- Prep: 1–2 weeks before:
  - Look up what your local recycling program takes. Many programs don't take plastic bags, and some don't take glass (check).
  - Bring a recycling bin, a trash can, a clean aluminum can with taped rims, and a few things made from recycled material, such as a recycled-paper notebook.
  - For the pots: one 6-inch pot per Lion, potting soil, small trowels (adults only), kid-size gloves and scoop cups for the Lions, table covers, a watering can, wet wipes and name labels.
  - Choose one native bee plant per Lion. The official page lists bee balm and coneflower among others. Good Georgia choices may include purple coneflower, bee balm, black-eyed Susan and asters (check with UGA Extension or a native-plant nursery).
  - The pots go where the leader chose (see Leader's choice). Option A: home, to a spot with 6 or more hours of sun (official page). Option B: the chartered organization (the local church), with its permission. Agree with the church on a sunny spot and who waters (check).
  - Confirm bee-sting allergies.
- Supplies: recycling bin, trash can, clean can, recycled-material items, pots, potting soil, trowels (adults only), kid-size gloves, scoop cups, native plants, table covers, watering can, wet wipes, name labels, stickers, crayons
- Tell parents before they leave: [Pots go home: Put your Lion's plant somewhere sunny and water it together. Bees need flowers!] [Pots stay at the church: Visit your Lion's plant at the church and watch for bees.] [Under Option A for Req 4: the April cleanup is [date/place] (check). Please return the permission slip and health form.]
1. **The Can's Big Trip** · den · 10 min · Reqs: 3
   - Say: "When we put a can in the recycling bin instead of the trash, it goes on a trip and comes back as something new. Let's act it out!"
   - How: 1) Drop the **can in the bin**. 2) Everyone is the **truck**: vroom around the room. 3) Everyone is the **sorting machine**: shake. 4) **Squish** it: crouch down. 5) **Melt** it: melt to the floor. 6) **New can**: jump up tall. 7) Do it once more with a **paper bag**.
   - Tip: **Trash or recycling?** Stuff in the trash usually goes to a landfill and doesn't come back.
2. **New From Old** · den · 7 min · Reqs: 3
   - Say: "These things were made from old things that got recycled. Can you guess what they used to be?"
   - How: 1) Show each **recycled item**, and the Lions guess. 2) Each pair picks **one thing to recycle** at home this week. 3) The Lion **tells the den**.
   - Tip: **Note Req 3** for each Lion who can tell you what recycling does, in their own words.
3. **Busy Bees** · den · 5 min · Reqs: 4 (set-up)
   - Say: "Bees visit flowers for food and carry pollen from flower to flower. That's how we get lots of fruits and vegetables! Flowers that grow wild here feed our bees best."
   - How: 1) Lions **buzz** from "flower" to "flower," which are partners holding up their hands. 2) Ask, "**What do bees need from us?**" Flowers!
   - Tip: **Bees aren't scary:** Bees on flowers are busy eating. Watch them, and don't swat.
4. **Bee Pots** · den · 13 min · Reqs: 4
   - Say: "Now we'll make a little bee restaurant!"
   - How: 1) Cover the **tables**. 2) Gloves on. The Lion **fills the pot halfway** with gloved hands or a cup while the partner works the trowel. 3) Set the **plant in**. 4) **Fill** around it and pat it down. 5) **Water** it. 6) The Lion decorates the **name label** with stickers or crayons.
   - Tip: **Note Req 4** (Option B) for each Lion who potted a plant with their partner. Lions use no tools here: only adults use the trowels.
5. **Clean-Up & Check-Out** · closing · 5 min · Reqs: none
   - Say: "Wipe your hands, then carry your bee restaurant with your grown-up."
   - How: 1) **Wipe hands** and wipe the tables. 2) Put spilled soil in the **food-scraps bin**. 3) **Check out** each Lion with their partner by name, pot in hand. If the pots stay at the church, an adult moves them to the agreed spot after check-out.
   - Tip: **Carry tray:** A shallow box keeps the pots upright in the car.
   - (at home) Water the plant and watch for bees, or visit it at the church.

### Meeting 3 of 3 · Clean Waterways Cleanup (Option A) (outing, about 55 min)
- All-dens option: this is the pack's April Scouting for Clean Waterways cleanup. Every den can come to the same site, and each den keeps its own two registered adults and its own zone.
- Prep: 3–4 weeks before:
  - Pick a site: a park, school ground or house of worship. It doesn't have to be at a creek (official Cub Scout page). For Lions, pick flat, dry ground away from roads and steep banks.
  - Get the landowner's permission, and ask whether they will lend bags or take the full bags (check).
  - Complete the Service Project Planning Checklist and the SAFE Checklist. Follow the council outing process (check).
  - Collect permission slips and every participant's health form, and review the health forms.
  - Walk the site first. Mark the boundaries, and look for poison ivy, fire-ant mounds, and broken glass or needles.
  - The adult in charge checks the weather the day before and that morning. Postpone for lightning or high heat.
- Supplies: work gloves for every Lion and adult, large trash bags, 1–3 five-gallon buckets for sharp items (official list), a site map, first-aid kit, each Lion's Six Essentials, extra water, hand sanitizer, sunscreen, health forms, a sign-in sheet
- Tell parents before they leave: Do a tick check tonight. Thank you for helping us serve!
1. **Check-In & Gloves** · den · 8 min · Reqs: 4 (set-up)
   - Say: "Gloves on, water bottles ready. Today we're helping nature!"
   - How: 1) **Sign in** each pair and collect any missing forms. 2) **Gloves** on everyone. 3) Take a **restroom stop**: a parent takes their own Lion or Tiger; otherwise buddies go together and an adult waits outside. 4) Show the **boundaries**.
   - Tip: **No partner, no Lion:** Every Lion needs their adult partner there for the whole cleanup.
2. **Touch or Tell?** · den · 7 min · Reqs: 4 (set-up)
   - Say: "Litter here can wash into drains and creeks, where fish and birds live. You pick up paper, plastic, wrappers and cans. For anything sharp, glass, heavy or yucky, shout 'Grown-up!' and don't touch."
   - How: 1) Hold up **examples**. 2) Lions shout "**Pick it up!**" or "**Grown-up!**" 3) Show the **sharps bucket**, which is for adults only.
   - Tip: **Needles:** Nobody touches them. An adult marks the spot and tells the landowner (check their procedure).
3. **Cleanup** · den · 25 min · Reqs: 4
   - Say: "Stay with your grown-up and inside our area. Let's see how much we can clean up!"
   - How: 1) Pairs work **side by side**. 2) The partner **holds the bag**, and the Lion picks up. 3) Adults carry the **sharps bucket**. 4) One leader stands at the **edge nearest any water or road**.
   - Tip: **Look before you reach:** Never reach under logs, into brush or into ant mounds.
4. **Water Break & Share** · den · 10 min · Reqs: 4
   - Say: "Look at all those bags! What was the strangest thing you found?"
   - How: 1) Everyone **drinks water** in the shade. 2) Gloves off, then **hand sanitizer**. 3) Each Lion **shares one find**. 4) Take a **den photo** with the bags. Photograph only scouts whose parents gave photo permission; post only on the pack's official channel, with no names.
   - Tip: **Note Req 4** (Option A) for each Lion who took part with their partner.
5. **Headcount & Check-Out** · closing · 5 min · Reqs: none
   - Say: "Last headcount! Thank you for being Champions for Nature."
   - How: 1) Count **every pair**. 2) An adult **hauls the bags** to the drop spot. 3) **Check out** each Lion with their partner by name.
   - Tip: **Tick reminder:** Say it to every partner. Log the project in Scoutbook for the certificate.

### Meeting 1 of 2 · Can Nature Make More? · 40 min
- All-dens option: Tiger and Wolf Reqs 1–2 are nearly identical, so the Tiger and Wolf dens can share Meeting 1. Use this plan or Wolf Meeting 1, with each den's own leaders running their own scouts.
- Prep: 1–2 weeks before:
  - Ask each pair to bring 2–3 small, clean items from home (official Renewable Show and Tell). Bring backups:
    - Renewable examples: wooden spoon, paper, cotton sock, wool hat, an apple.
    - Nonrenewable examples: plastic bottle (made from oil), rinsed aluminum can with taped rim (mined metal), and pictures of coal and a gas pump.
  - Make two signs: "Nature makes more" (a sprouting seed) and "Nature can't make more fast" (a clock).
  - Set up a whiteboard or flip chart and markers.
- Supplies: backup items, 2 signs, whiteboard or flip chart, markers, stopwatch, crayons
- Tell parents before they leave: This week, count how many things your family reuses. Meeting 2 is [date]. We make planters from 2-liter bottles, so please save one clean bottle, label off.
1. **Can Nature Make More?** · den · 8 min · Reqs: 1

### Meeting 2 of 3 · Soil and Air Lab · 40 min

   - (at home) Option B: hang the cards and write down where.
